INDEPENDENT ADVERSARIAL REVIEW: v0.5 CORRECTION-AWARE INFERENCE

Review date: 2026-10-02.
Status: completed implementation review and independent finite-ledger audit.

Scope

The reviewer inspected decoder.py, coupled.py, pipeline.py, corrections.py,
correction_io.py, correction_pipeline.py, their authored diagnostic tests,
and the previous implementation-review report. A final sanity pass also read
run_correction_source_demo.py and its source-only annotation/evidence capsules.
No natural development gold, evaluation labels, or benchmark answers were read. The review
concerns implementation semantics, not learned extraction accuracy, calibration,
source entailment, algorithmic novelty, or a natural joint-inference advantage.

Pre-implementation findings and required counterexamples

1. A correcting assertion can replace a wrong effective start while retaining
   the same factual value. If an old RESTATES equality rejects inconsistent exact
   starts before correction projection, a later materializer hook cannot repair
   feasibility. All global temporal checks must operate on the active projection.

2. For C correcting B, which corrected A, neither A nor B should reappear after
   B is withdrawn. The supported operation is full assertion replacement with
   persistent correction notices. Retraction of a correction notice and implicit
   resurrection are different operations and need separate semantics.

3. A later report is not a correction merely because it disagrees or was
   published later. A correction candidate needs an exact declared target,
   a correction cue, and the supported authority relation. Presence of spans
   and metadata is not an independent proof that the source entails them.
   Same-day source availability cannot establish ordering on its own.

4. Replacement scope must be exactly the addressed atomic assertion. A partial
   correction must not silently erase all facts for a key, source, episode, or
   history. Unsupported partial replacement should fail closed.

5. RESTATES expresses same-episode identity, not copied evidence. A corrected
   assertion must not automatically withdraw every corroborating assertion in
   its equality component. Cascading withdrawal requires a separately declared
   essential support dependency. Absence of such a declaration does not prove
   that the sources are independent in the world.

6. Essential support dependencies must cascade transitively, with explicit cycle
   handling. A dependent selected reading cannot silently survive when its
   required target reading was unselected, unresolved, or withdrawn. Conjunctive
   essential dependencies and disjunctive alternative support must not be
   conflated; only the former is proposed for this checkpoint.

7. Withdrawal must remove the target's bounds, observations, incident temporal
   links, and assertion-level answer support. Its identity may remain in a
   separate correction-lineage audit. Knowledge-date availability must never be
   inserted as an event-time transition or an appointment date.

8. Competing sibling corrections must remain competing claims. Selecting a
   correction edge does not permit recency to choose between equally supported
   replacements, and score-optimal selection does not establish factual truth.

9. The shared decoder hook must replace, rather than merely add to, the old
   global feasibility checks. Its returned active components must contain only
   selected resolved mentions, without duplicates or mutable containers. The
   same semantics must apply to independent, iterative, restarted, exact, and
   assignment-evaluation paths. A malformed hook result must raise instead of
   silently rejecting a candidate state.

Executed findings

1. The shared state_semantics decoder extension now runs after local candidate
   identity checks and before every legacy global temporal check. The old
   Boolean extension and new replacement extension cannot be combined. Returned
   components must be immutable, canonically ordered, and contain distinct,
   selected resolved mentions. Malformed returns raise instead of silently
   becoming infeasible. The same extension is passed by all four pipeline
   methods and by evaluate_assignment.

2. scripts/audit_correction_finite.py independently enumerated every subset of
   four assertions. The expected active ledger is the unique greatest subset
   that excludes every selected correction target and satisfies every essential
   support implication. This is a finite enumeration oracle, rather than a
   copy of production's iterative withdrawal computation.

   There were 576 chronological correction/support DAG pairs and 384 essential
   support DAG cases with varying selected/unresolved prerequisites. The audit
   compared active claim IDs, envelope IDs, and assertion-level answer support
   over eight event days. It encountered 171 empty active ledgers among the
   correction/support DAG pairs. All expected ledgers matched.

   Twelve additional targeted checks exercised the old pre-hook RESTATES rejection
   versus correction-aware acceptance, removal of incident equality constraints,
   retained independent support, explicitly dependent support withdrawal,
   conflicting sibling corrections, unrelated asserting authorities, and
   unsupported partial coverage, and the context needed to suppress a rival when
   its essential prerequisite was left unresolved. Eight weighted objective
   decomposition checks were also executed. Total: 10,772 checks, zero mismatches.
   The reproducible report is results/correction_finite_audit_v05.json.

3. All 52 correction-policy and correction-cache unit tests present at the final
   of review passed. These included same-day references, correction cycles,
   nested replacement without resurrection, independent corroboration versus
   essential derivation, a newly asserted reinstated value, planned correction
   modality, malformed evidence, prefix constraints, immutable policies, stale
   hashes, strict schema boundaries, and preservation of nested v0.4 lineage.

4. correction_pipeline.py and correction_io.py were inspected. The outer cache
   identity hashes the base candidate cache, correction policy, and annotation
   provenance. An additional executed pipeline check used the authored cache-test
   fixture with all four methods: independent, iterative, restarted, and exact.
   Each selected objective 5.0 and returned Bea with only assertion c2 as support.
   Changing only the policy while keeping the same base cache caused each
   previously decoded state to be rejected by answer_corrected. This checks
   shared semantics and policy identity; it is not a comparative accuracy result.

5. The objective decomposition was checked using a separately authored fixed
   assignment with beta=2, nonzero violation penalties, a resolved active claim,
   withdrawn claim, unresolved reading, active temporal link, inactive temporal
   link, correction link, and nonzero null scores. The seven net contributions
   were respectively 9.7, 0.9, 6.9, 11.8, 9.7, 7.6, and 0.7, summing to 47.3.
   Explicit expected arithmetic matched every reported bucket and the decoder's
   assignment evaluator. Inactive contributions are accurately disclosed rather
   than silently presented as active-answer utility. This probe is preserved in
   the independent audit script and its JSON result.

6. A provenance counterexample was discovered and executed during final review.
   A rival claim depended on a prerequisite whose unresolved alternative used a
   separate context source. Removing that rival made another answer certain,
   but the first implementation omitted the prerequisite's source and the context
   behind the unresolved choice. The corrected implementation includes the
   prerequisite source, mention context, selected alternative reading context,
   and selected alternative claim context when present. These controls propagate
   through essential dependency chains. The independent counterexample now
   passes, and two additional unit regressions cover unresolved and resolved
   alternative readings. No withdrawn assertion is restored as factual support.

7. The real-source demonstration was sanity-checked as a record-ledger
   illustration. It preserves the approximate FY2024 forecast modality, unknown
   calendar bounds and absence of daily observations. Both certificate variants
   declare the same complete input bundle, candidate records, and authored
   scores. The source dateline remains distinct from current capture availability.
   The runner makes no QA call, historical-availability claim, learned-score
   claim, or method-superiority claim. Compact exact excerpts preserve offset
   maps, but target and authority semantics remain conditional on the retained
   full-input annotation; compact spans alone do not independently certify them.

Semantic boundaries and remaining research limitations

The implemented correction is an atomic full-assertion replacement notice. Its
withdrawal effect persists even if the replacement assertion is subsequently
withdrawn or unsupported. This prevents ancestor resurrection; it does not
implement retracting a notice, partial endpoint edits, field-level repairs, or
disjunctive alternative derivations. Essential dependencies are conjunctive and
candidate-specific. A missing selected prerequisite makes its dependent inactive,
maintaining a feasible null-link state without inventing supporting evidence.

Withdrawing an assertion removes its temporal constraints and assertion-level
answer support. Source-level control or extraction context can remain cited for
another reason. The target source can still establish the notice's identified
target and asserting authority. A later-withdrawn replacement may itself have
issued a still-operative correction notice. The implementation retains these
sources as control provenance. Same-key correction-control dependencies are
conservative and can overcite notices from unrelated episodes. Retention as
context/control evidence must not be described as continuing factual support
from the withdrawn assertion.

Exact quotes establish that an annotated span occurs in a source. They do not
prove that a span identifies the correct target or asserting authority. Authority
is a declared same-origin relation, not an independently verified trust score.
The source-level authority model also cannot express multiple asserting origins
inside one document without additional representation work. Sources with no
declared derivative dependency are retained; this does not prove their real-world
independence. These assumptions remain conditional input annotations.

The shared objective continues scoring the selected historical interpretations,
including readings and temporal links whose active constraints were withdrawn.
This is internally consistent across all methods, but can reward inactive links
or favor choosing corrections that relax a difficult temporal interpretation.
A score increase therefore does not establish a better supported active answer.
The implementation now exposes active/inactive objective contributions. Empirical
work should additionally compare an active-support scoring ablation before
attributing gains to correction-aware inference. The review does not validate
the quality or calibration of the scores.

Review disposition: no high-priority implementation blocker was found in the
reviewed scope after the global-check ordering and absence-provenance fixes. Finite generated coverage is
not a proof for arbitrary graphs. No model extraction, natural gold, benchmark
result, learned-score benefit, or publishable joint-inference advantage was
established by this review.
