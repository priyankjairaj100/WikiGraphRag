Core certificate theory working note v0.21
Authored 2026-10-05; mathematical development, not an empirical result.

Scope and status

This note supplies definitions and proofs for a finite candidate/witness
certificate. It does not implement a natural-language proposer, semantic
verifier, coverage oracle or source-authenticity checker. The claims below are
conditional mathematical statements. No novelty, prevalence, effectiveness,
natural-data improvement, calibration or publishability claim follows. No
model/native execution or private-source restoration was performed for this
note. Existing v14/v16 statements and failed experiments remain unchanged.

The proofs are given here in full rather than attributed to a new algorithmic
discovery. Related-work attribution and optimization guarantees belong in the
separate synthesis. The inspected local contracts are
docs/method_hypothesis_v14.txt, docs/witness_selection_v14.txt,
docs/algorithmic_next_stage_v16_proposal.txt and
src/temporal_state/witness_selection_v14.py. The existing code checks supplied
maps; it does not establish the semantic assumptions of these theorems.

1. Task contract, full joint answers and answer equivalence

Fix a question q, immutable source corpus D and declared interpretation policy
P. P fixes the admissible query readings, source authority rules, document
identity/version rules, permissible concept mappings, inference rules and
normalizations. These cannot be changed after seeing a desired answer. D means
the admitted source corpus at the declared cut-off, not all information that
could exist. The assurance is source-relative; it does not prove the sources
factually correct, authentic, or first publicly available at their stated dates.

Let R_q be the finite ordered set of required output slots. A slot can request a
reported result from a specified source version, or an explicit comparison.
A complete answer contains every required slot. A reported-result slot is the
record

 (slot, value, concept, entity, period, unit/scale, population,
  analysis definition, source-version identity).

The period distinguishes an instant from an interval and distinguishes reporting
from observation periods where relevant. Population includes inclusion,
consolidation or dimensional restrictions demanded by q. Version identity is
the declared immutable document/source identity, not merely a displayed date.
An exact value representation and any allowed normalization are fixed by P.
An unknown material binding is not a completed factual slot.

A comparison slot records its two endpoint slots, relation type and all material
qualifiers. For example, reporting two scoped values, reporting their arithmetic
difference, asserting a comparable change, asserting an explicit correction and
asserting a changed-scope reanalysis are different actions. Equal numbers,
recency, silence in a newer source, or a shared row label prove none of these
relations. Arithmetic is licensed only by a registered checked derivation from
supported operands; a semantic relation additionally needs its own premises.

A hypothesis h is a complete JOINT proposal for all slots, plus a nonempty finite
set C(h) of necessary source claims and a proposed binding/derivation structure.
Every output assertion and necessary entity/concept/period/unit/population/
definition/version attachment must occur in C(h) or have an explicit finite
derivation from it. Necessary comparison premises cannot be omitted. An ordinary
record extracted from one version is a component of h, not a competing whole
answer to a question requesting two versions. Explicit question conditions are
hard eligibility constraints; proposing a relaxed condition does not satisfy q.

Let K_q(h) assert that h is structurally complete and eligible under q and P.
K_q includes faithful query parsing and derivation/output type checking; these
are substantive assumptions, not services supplied by a document hash. The
abstract universe U contains every eligible anchored joint interpretation in
the declared task domain. It need not be enumerable or finite. The actual
proposal set H is a nonempty finite subset of U. Requiring H subset U means a
structural validator has rejected malformed proposals. If that validator is
fallible, its error is separately accounted for in Section 7.

The answer map a_q(h) is the canonical joint record emitted by h. It preserves
every requested source-version slot and comparison relation. Harmless parsing
differences or alternative supporting citations may map to the same record;
source identity is never discarded just because values agree. Define

 h ~_q g  iff  a_q(h) = a_q(g),
 I_q(h,g) = 1 iff h is not ~_q g.

Equality of these canonical records is an equivalence relation: reflexivity,
symmetry and transitivity follow from equality. The quotient H/~_q partitions
candidates into answer-changing classes. A declared equivalence mapping must
preserve q-relevant distinctions; a model-supplied symmetric incompatibility
graph alone need not be the complement of such an equivalence relation. In
particular, nontransitive pairwise 'compatible enough' judgments do not define
this quotient. An arbitrary graph can still be used as a heuristic objective,
but the theorem below requires an exact answer map, or the stronger direct
condition that no distinct scoped actions are ever marked compatible.

Example. A question requests revenue in original version V1 and amended version
V2. The full answer ((V1,12,...),(V2,12,...)) has two slots. It is not equivalent
to ((V2,12,...)) or to an unversioned '12'. If the population in V1 is a segment
and that in V2 is consolidated, equal values still do not support a comparable
change. A candidate proposing 'explicit correction' and a candidate merely
reporting the two results are different joint actions.

2. Source semantics and what positive support means

Use a fixed, coherent semantic interpretation of the source assertions. One
precise realization is a nonempty class M(D) of models satisfying D under P.
Source assertions are provenance-scoped: 'V1 reports 12' and 'V2 reports 15' do
not create a logical contradiction. If assertions actually conflict, P must
represent that conflict without classical explosion; alternatively the affected
task is outside the coherent-model theorem until the conflict is resolved.
It is not legitimate to delete one source implicitly to recover consistency.

Each c in C(h) denotes a precise proposition. Write D |= c when every model in
M(D) satisfies c. Define phi_h = conjunction of c in C(h), and define

 T = {h in U : some m in M(D) satisfies phi_h},
 W = {h in U : D |= phi_h}.

T is the set of source-consistent eligible joint interpretations. W is the set
of positively warranted complete joint proposals. Since M(D) is nonempty,
W subset T. The resolution theorems concern tasks with nonempty T. If no
eligible interpretation exists, faithful sound positive support cannot exist
and the system must abstain or report the task inconsistency. A task can have
more than one source-consistent answer class; this
includes ambiguity in an admitted query reading and unresolved source bindings.
The certificate will guarantee both positive warrant and a unique action over
T. It does not merely pick one plausible member of T.

This distinction matters logically. If 'positive support' were DEFINED to mean
'the complete correct and unique answer to q in every admissible interpretation',
positive support alone would already establish the desired conclusion. A
coverage/elimination theorem would then be redundant. Here support warrants the
claims of a complete eligible proposal. Uniqueness across all admissible query
resolutions is a separate claim. For a task whose exact formal semantics already
makes any fully supported eligible action uniquely correct, coverage is not
necessary for factual correctness. We do not claim an impossibility that
contradicts that stronger assumption.

An action can also be correct relative to one externally fixed reference
interpretation t*. Section 5 gives the weaker target-preservation theorem. This
must not be confused with certifying the absence of all other admissible actions.

3. Anchors, conjunctive witnesses and three-valued judgments

A source span consists of immutable source identity, exact offset/coordinates and
text identity. Let cov(E) be the union of spans actually delivered in evidence
selection E. A rule w is

 (rule ID, h, c, sign, D_w, derivation/provenance),

where c is in C(h), sign is + or -, and D_w is a nonempty finite set of required
source spans. The rule activates iff EVERY span in D_w is fully covered by
cov(E). Bundle names do not activate rules. Shared spans can be supplied by
different bundles. If a derivation has intermediate premises, its flattened
leaf dependencies include every required source span and every non-source
axiom is declared in P. A generated explanation is not an additional axiom.

Rule soundness means that the anchored source assertions jointly entail c for
sign +, or entail not-c for sign -, under P. Faithful source extraction makes
those assertions consequences of D. Consequently a sound active + rule implies
D |= c, and a sound active - rule implies D |= not-c. Checking span presence or
hashes proves neither entailment. An apparent structural mismatch refutes a
candidate only when the relevant exclusivity/type/attachment premise is itself
justified. A row cannot be declared to have exactly one parent merely because
that assumption makes an alternative easy to reject.

Let P_E(h,c) mean at least one active + rule exists and N_E(h,c) mean at least one
active - rule exists. Define the three-valued state

 supported     if P_E and not N_E;
 contradicted  if N_E and not P_E;
 unresolved    otherwise.

Additionally record a conflict flag when P_E and N_E both hold. Thus unresolved
is not silently identified with conflict: the public inference has three truth
statuses and a separate diagnostic flag. This matches v14. An explicit
'unresolved' record adds no positive or negative conclusion. Source absence,
retrieval failure, incomplete anchors and verifier uncertainty do not create a
negative rule. A rule with two dependencies does not activate from either one.

Define

 Ref_E(h) iff some c in C(h) has state contradicted;
 Pos_E(h) iff every c in C(h) has state supported;
 V_E = {h in H : not Ref_E(h)}.

Positive support is conjunctive across the required claims; a contradiction of
one necessary claim is sufficient to refute the whole candidate. Pos_E(h)
implies h in V_E. A conflict anywhere in the active supplied map blocks a v14
certificate. This global rule is conservative even when the conflict concerns
a rival already refuted on another claim.

Lemma 1 (local soundness). If all active rules are semantically sound and M(D)
is nonempty, then (i) no active claim has a + / - conflict, (ii) Pos_E(h) implies
h in W, and (iii) Ref_E(h) implies h not in T.

Proof. A conflict would yield both D |= c and D |= not-c, impossible in any
nonempty M(D). If every required claim is supported, every model satisfies
every conjunct, so D |= phi_h. If a necessary c is contradicted, every model
satisfies not-c; none can satisfy phi_h, which includes c. QED.

The rule premise is only sufficient for this local lemma. For certificate
soundness it is enough that the support rules actually used for the selected
winner are sound and that the negative rules used to eliminate relevant valid
rivals do not falsely refute them. Blanket accuracy of every possible judgment
is stronger than necessary. Section 7 localizes the error event accordingly.

4. The unknown sentinel and valid class coverage

The semantic candidate-coverage property is

 COV(H;D,q) : for every t in T there exists h in H intersect T
              such that a_q(h) = a_q(t).

This requires at least one SOURCE-CONSISTENT representative of every admissible
answer class. It does not require every harmless parse. It is weaker than
enumerating all of T but stronger than merely seeing each action label in H.
An invalid representative can be soundly refuted, so action-label coverage by
invalid representatives is insufficient. Coverage of one reference answer is
also weaker than coverage of all admissible classes when the task is ambiguous.

The operational unknown sentinel has state OPEN or COVERED. It starts OPEN.
COVERED requires a coverage assessment/proof with a declared domain and
provenance. No selected source witness, low pair loss, beam exhaustion, duplicate
removal, confidence score, or lack of another proposed answer clears it by
itself. In a formally finite source grammar, exhaustive enumeration plus sound
grammar-completeness assumptions may establish COV. On unrestricted natural
language, an audit or calibrated estimator may supply fallible assurance; that
is an empirical coverage policy, not an unconditional proof.

Write hat-COV for the operational decision to set COVERED. Coverage-sentinel
soundness is the implication hat-COV => COV. Independent justification means
the assertion is not inferred circularly from the certificate it enables; it
does not require statistical independence between the proposer and verifier.
The v14 provenance string documents an assessment but does not prove this
implication. OPEN is not an ordinary low-weight hypothesis that can be removed
by contradicting one finite candidate. Assigning it zero weight changes no
logical requirement.

5. Certificate definition and soundness

Let B be the declared final evidence budget and cost(E) the exact declared
rendering cost, including required source labels and prompt overhead. A
certificate consists of E, a winner h*, one active support derivation for each
c in C(h*), an active contradiction derivation for every rival candidate, and a
coverage assessment. Its gate is

 (G1) cost(E) <= B;
 (G2) there is no active claim conflict;
 (G3) Pos_E(h*);
 (G4) for every g in H with a_q(g) != a_q(h*), Ref_E(g);
 (G5) hat-COV is true.

G4 quantifies over EVERY candidate in EVERY rival class. Refuting one
representative of a class does not refute the class. A class-level witness is
allowed only if it has a sound universal argument covering all its members.
Equivalent candidates need not all be supported or refuted. Their harmless
remaining ambiguity does not change the requested output.

G3 implies that at least one survivor remains. Under the exact equivalence map,
G3 and G4 are equivalent to: at least one fully supported survivor and every
survivor has the same action as that supported survivor. There is no need to
prove every surviving harmless parse. This is the precise positive-support AND
all-rival-refutation condition; a zero numerical objective is not the gate.

Theorem 1 (source-relative certificate soundness). Suppose K_q and a_q are
faithful, source extraction/identity and the output renderer are faithful,
M(D) is nonempty, all active rules are semantically sound, and the coverage
sentinel is sound. If G1--G5 hold, then h* in W and

 {a_q(t) : t in T} = {a_q(h*)}.

Therefore the emitted action is a complete, positively warranted, uniquely
resolved joint answer in the declared source/query domain. Every requested
version and required comparison is retained. This statement says nothing about
undeclared sources or the real-world truth of reported assertions.

Proof. By G3 and Lemma 1, h* lies in W and hence T, so the action set on the
left is nonempty and contains a_q(h*). Let t be any member of T. G5 and sentinel
soundness provide h in H intersect T with a_q(h)=a_q(t). If this action differed
from a_q(h*), G4 would give Ref_E(h), and Lemma 1 would give h not in T, a
contradiction. Thus every t has the winner's action. The positive derivations
establish each required claim of h*, faithful K_q supplies all required slots,
and faithful rendering cannot introduce new claims. G1 establishes only the
declared input-cost condition, independently of the semantic argument. QED.

Theorem 2 (target-preservation version). Fix a reference t* in T. Replace full
COV in Theorem 1 by the weaker requirement that some h in H intersect T has
a_q(h)=a_q(t*). Under the other assumptions, an accepted action equals
a_q(t*), and its required claims are positively warranted. This does not prove
there are no other admissible actions in T.

Proof. The same contradiction argument applies only to the given valid
representative of t*. Positive warrant still follows from Lemma 1. Nothing in
this weaker premise provides a representative for other classes, so no
uniqueness conclusion follows. QED.

Necessary distinction. Under a predeclared uniquely correct target and a
positive oracle that already proves full task correctness, Theorem 2's coverage
condition is sufficient but may not be necessary. A theorem need not have
logically minimal assumptions. Claims of necessity below concern the stronger
unique-resolution guarantee with only source-claim support, not an omniscient
full-answer oracle.

6. Exact finite checking and conditional completeness

Let A be a finite set of allowed evidence bundles and J a finite rule list. A
selection E is a subset of A; its actual received spans are the union of bundle
spans. The union may satisfy a rule whose dependencies cross multiple bundles.
No rule can be partially activated. Let F_B be the finite set of selections
satisfying G1--G5 for some winner. An exhaustive decision procedure enumerates
all subsets of A, computes exact received-span coverage and rule states, and
checks the gates using the exact answer map.

Theorem 3 (finite-map decision completeness). For fixed supplied H, A, J,
hat-COV, rendering and exact cost, exhaustive checking returns a certificate iff
F_B is nonempty. It returns none iff no certificate expressible by those supplied
objects fits B.

Proof. Every possible E is enumerated exactly as an element of the finite power
set of A. The checker implements the defining predicates of F_B. If it returns
one, that member satisfies the predicates. If F_B is nonempty, its members
occur in the enumeration and at least one passes. QED.

This theorem concerns supplied maps, not truth. The v14 tiny exhaustive routine
with require_answerable=True provides this guarantee only for its admitted
bundle cap, supplied incompatibility map, supplied counter and its own gates.
A heuristic's failure does not establish F_B empty. Minimizing pair loss
without requiring answerability is not this decision problem.

Define B* = min{cost(E): E satisfies G2--G5 for some h*}, with B*=infinity if
the set is empty. Because A is finite, every finite minimum is attained. With
the other supplied objects fixed, a certificate exists at budget B iff B>=B*.
This remains true even if merging source labels makes the exact cost
nonmonotone. It does not imply that minimum-cost search is computationally easy.

Theorem 4 (conditional semantic completeness). Assume the soundness premises
of Theorem 1 and that the source-consistent action set T/~_q has exactly one
class C*. Further assume:

 (C1) the operational coverage sentinel is COVERED and is justified;
 (C2) some h* in H with action C* has a sound positive witness for every
      necessary claim, expressible in the admitted rule/bundle vocabulary;
 (C3) for every g in H with action outside C*, at least one necessary claim
      has a sound negative witness in that vocabulary;
 (C4) there is one admitted selection E0 containing the dependencies of all
      chosen witnesses, with cost(E0)<=B; all additional active judgments
      are conflict-free (automatic under global coherent rule soundness);
 (C5) selection/search is complete over the admitted finite selections.

Then the procedure returns a certificate and its answer has action C*.

Proof. C4 activates all support witnesses from C2 and all contradiction
witnesses from C3. Soundness and coherence prevent an opposing active rule from
turning one of these verdicts into a conflict. Thus E0 satisfies G1--G4; C1
supplies G5. F_B is nonempty. C5 and Theorem 3 return a member; Theorem 1 proves
the returned action is the unique class C*. QED.

Each extra condition is substantive. Semantic uniqueness alone does not imply
that the verifier vocabulary can express the required proof, that retrieval
offers its dependencies, that the budget fits their union, that a learned
verifier will recognize it, or that a greedy search will find it. C2/C3 can be
called finite witness completeness only in a declared source/query calculus;
there is no completeness theorem here for an unrestricted language model.
C4 is a JOINT budget condition. Individually affordable witnesses need not
coexist within B. Witness overlap can reduce cost but cannot erase dependencies.
Unknown coverage can correctly force abstention even when a true answer exists.

Projected partial answers. A fixed projection pi onto a declared subset of
requested slots admits the same theorem using a'_q=pi composed with a_q and
complete support for the emitted projected claims. Alternatives differing on
unreported slots then need not be separated. Such a certificate is explicitly
a partial answer; it does not establish the withheld comparison or silently
remove a requested source version from the full-answer endpoint. The target
of the theorem must be stated before interpreting an evaluation metric.

7. Error decomposition and selective risk

The preceding statements are deterministic. For probabilistic analysis, draw
a task/run X from a declared population; include proposer/verifier randomness,
adaptive retrieval and rendering in the run. Let A_emit be the event the system
emits a complete answer, and let Bad be failure of the complete grounded unique-
resolution conclusion of Theorem 1. For a unique-reference metric one may use
Theorem 2's target-coverage event instead, but must label the changed endpoint.

First retain the nonempty coherent M(D) and eligible-domain setup, and assume
faithful query/action/schema representation, source extraction, budget
accounting and rendering. Require every emitted run to pass the finite
mechanical certificate gate G1--G5 and to retain its selected support and
refutation proofs. This is a protocol assumption, not an assumption that its
semantic judgments or coverage assertion are correct. Their truth may fail
and is captured by the following error events. An ungated arbitrary emitter
does not satisfy the risk theorem. On emitted runs define:

 E_C : the sentinel was cleared although COV is false;
 E_+ : at least one positive judgment used in the chosen complete support proof
       is semantically false;
 E_- : at least one negative judgment used to eliminate a source-consistent
       rival candidate is semantically false.

The semantic failure events concern the actual selected proof. A false
negative in this naming is an erroneous contradiction/elimination, not a
failure to recognize support. A failure to recognize a true support may cause
abstention but does not itself violate accepted-answer soundness. To avoid
terminological ambiguity, reports should say 'false support' and 'false
elimination' rather than rely only on positive/negative labels.

Theorem 5 (failure localization). With the assumed exact representations and
mechanically gated emission protocol, but without assuming semantic correctness
of the supplied judgments or coverage assertion,

 A_emit intersect Bad subset E_C union E_+ union E_-.

Proof. Consider an emitted run outside this union. The selected positive proof
is sound, so its complete eligible winner is warranted. Coverage holds. If
there were a source-consistent action different from the winner, coverage
would supply a source-consistent candidate in its class. The certificate gate
requires this candidate to be refuted. Its used negative judgment would then
be false, yielding E_-, a contradiction. Hence the emitted output is grounded
and unique. QED.

Put E_V=E_+ union E_-. An exact partition followed by upper bounds gives

 P(A_emit and Bad)
   = P(A_emit and Bad and E_C)
     + P(A_emit and Bad and not E_C and E_V)
   <= P(A_emit and E_C) + P(A_emit and not E_C and E_V)
   <= P(E_C) + P(E_+) + P(E_-).

The equality uses Theorem 5: the residual part outside E_C union E_V is zero.
No independence assumption is used. This is a decomposition/bound on JOINT
error probability (unsupported or unresolved emitted answers per all tasks).

Let p=P(A_emit)>0. Selective risk, the error rate AMONG emitted answers, is

 R_sel = P(Bad | A_emit)
       = P(A_emit and Bad)/p
       <= min(1, [P(E_C)+P(E_+)+P(E_-)]/p).

If p=0 selective risk is undefined, not zero accuracy loss. An always-abstain
system has zero joint error and zero answer coverage; it has not solved QA.
Bounds on marginal proposal omissions or marginal verifier errors cannot be
reported directly as bounds on R_sel without this denominator or conditional
calibration. For example, p=0.01 with every emitted answer wrong yields joint
error 0.01 and selective risk 1. All failures can concentrate on selected runs.

If directly measured conditional bounds are available, let

 delta_C = P(E_C | A_emit),
 delta_V = P(E_V | A_emit and not E_C).

When both conditioning events have positive probability,

 R_sel <= delta_C + (1-delta_C)*delta_V.

If the second event has probability zero its contribution is zero and no
conditional value need be defined. This formula is again a partition identity
plus an inequality, not an independence model. Estimates of these population
quantities need their own sampling assumptions and confidence intervals;
the deterministic theorem supplies no estimates.

Representation and rendering are not silently absorbed into a favorable
verifier metric. Let E_R cover a wrong query parse, omitted required claim,
incorrect answer-equivalence mapping, unsupported concept normalization,
source/extraction/anchor semantics error, or renderer adding/changing a claim.
With these components fallible the bound becomes

 A_emit and Bad subset E_R union E_C union E_+ union E_-,

and the corresponding marginal or selective bound gains the E_R term. Source
authenticity/external real-world truth is outside Bad unless explicitly added
to the target; expanding the target requires new assumptions and error terms.
This is why a two-term candidate/verifier bound is valid only after declaring
which other components are exact or included in the verifier event.

Claimwise accuracy is not runwise verifier soundness. A run may use many
judgments. If at most K adaptive calls are made, and for EVERY reachable prior
history the next issued judgment has conditional semantic-error probability
at most eta, then the probability of any erroneous call is at most K*eta
(capped at 1). Proof: condition on each history, bound each issued error
indicator's expectation by eta, and apply the union bound. A predictable
bounded stopping rule likewise gives eta*E[number of issued calls] under the
same uniform conditional assumption. It is not enough to know the average
error on a static easy call set; adaptive selection may target a high-error
subset. Using all issued calls for this bound is conservative but avoids
post-selection conditioning on favorable judgments. No independence of calls
is needed for the union bound.

8. Counterexamples and impossibility boundaries

8.1 Missing valid-class coverage defeats unique-resolution certification.

Use the same observable finite transcript H={h_a}, a fully supported h_a,
no rival candidates and an unjustified cleared sentinel. In source/query
completion W0, T={h_a}. In completion W1, T={h_a,h_b}, where h_b has a different
eligible scoped action, all claims supporting h_a remain true, and the
additional source/query possibility was not represented or examined. The
finite transcript and every judgment on H are identical. Any deterministic
procedure whose decision uses only this transcript emits the same unique
answer in both; its uniqueness conclusion is
false in W1. A randomized procedure cannot eliminate the indistinguishability:
conditioned on the same internal randomness it takes the same action. If it
emits with probability r on this transcript, it makes a false uniqueness claim
with probability r in W1. Universal soundness requires abstention on that
transcript or an additional sound coverage/uniqueness premise.

This example does not make h_a's positively warranted facts false. It proves
necessity of additional information for the unique-resolution claim. If a
fixed single-target task and a perfect full-correctness positive oracle are
assumed instead, claiming that coverage is necessary for correctness would be
wrong. If support checks only bare numerical facts and omits a necessary scope
binding, a missing correct candidate can also cause an actually wrong answer,
but that example additionally violates the full positive-support contract and
must be labeled as such.

8.2 Covering action labels with invalid representatives is insufficient.

Let T contain valid t_a and t_b with distinct actions a and b. H contains valid
h_a with action a and invalid g_b with action b. A source-sound contradiction
refutes g_b; h_a is supported. The set of action labels a(H) contains both true
labels, yet the certificate would falsely claim uniqueness of a if this weak
label-only condition cleared the sentinel. Proper COV fails because no valid
representative of b is in H. This is not verifier error.

8.3 A syntactic anchor check cannot substitute for semantic verifier soundness.

Take a coherent source whose single relevant reported result is 7. H contains
complete h_7 and h_9, coverage is complete, and their actions differ. Attach
valid source offsets/hashes to false rules '+ h_9 value=9' and '- h_7 value=7'.
The finite gate accepts h_9 with every syntactic span requirement satisfied.
The output is wrong. A checker that only sees source identity, span coverage
and supplied labels has no guarantee against this construction. Checking the
source meaning would be a semantic verifier and would restore the missing
assumption. Correct byte identity never implies semantic correctness.

For a narrower false-elimination-only example, let two different query readings
both have genuine supported facts. Falsely refuting one supported rival makes
a falsely unique answer possible while the selected winner's support remains
true. Thus evaluating only positive claim support cannot validate elimination.

8.4 Elimination alone is insufficient.

H={h_a,h_b}; h_b is soundly contradicted, h_a has no positive support for a
necessary unit or population binding. There is a singleton survivor and zero
incompatible-pair loss, but no complete grounded answer. Alternatively both
candidates are refuted, producing zero loss with no survivor. G3 rejects both
cases. Even positive fixed weights do not repair missing positive support.

8.5 Refuting one representative does not refute an answer class.

H={h_a,g_b1,g_b2}, with a(g_b1)=a(g_b2)=b different from a. The source refutes
g_b1's proposed continuation link but g_b2 uses another still-valid link and
has the same final scoped action. A supported h_a plus refutation of g_b1
does not establish uniqueness; g_b2 survives. G4 therefore quantifies over all
rival candidates or requires a genuinely class-universal rule.

8.6 Conjunctive dependency truncation creates false certificates.

A support rule for 'the value is in millions' requires both a continuation
identity linking the row to table T and T's unit header. The row fragment can
instead be attached to a thousands table when the continuation identity is
missing. If the rule is activated from the unit header alone, the same delivered
snippet is consistent with different bindings. The rule's nominal correctness
on its FULL dependency set does not save activation on a strict subset. A
certificate must include all premises, or leave that claim unresolved.

8.7 Joint versions cannot be represented as rivals to be eliminated.

The source validly reports 12 in V1 and 15 in V2, and q requests both. A
candidate set {h_V1,h_V2} with one single-version answer per candidate is
structurally incomplete. Eliminating either is inappropriate even if the later
document is authoritative for a different task. The proper hypotheses contain
both requested records; alternatives differ in bindings/comparison relations
within that joint object. Treating the versions as rivals violates K_q and
cannot be repaired by a perfectly accurate selector.

8.8 Zero weighted ambiguity is not logical agreement.

If incompatible surviving candidates have weights w_a>0 and w_b=0, their
fixed pair product is zero. A pair-loss objective therefore reports zero while
the logical ambiguity remains. Even strictly positive weights yield zero loss
when all candidates are eliminated. The logical gate is independent of weights.

8.9 Conflicts defeat unconditional monotonicity.

With predicted rules, a small E can activate a negative judgment on h. Adding
evidence can activate a positive judgment on the same claim. The v14 conflict
policy changes the claim from contradicted to unresolved, resurrects h, and
blocks an answer. Pair loss can increase. Therefore unconditional monotone
survivor elimination or monotone answerability is false for the supplied-map
scaffold. Under globally sound rules and coherent semantics, Lemma 1 excludes
conflicts; activated negative conclusions then persist, so survivors can only
shrink as spans are added. Budget feasibility still need not be preserved.

9. Precisely what remains unproved or unimplemented

The rigorous result is a certificate contract plus conditional soundness,
conditional witness/search completeness and an error-event decomposition. It
