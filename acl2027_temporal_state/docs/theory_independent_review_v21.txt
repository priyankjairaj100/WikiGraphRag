Current finding

No unresolved mathematical blocker was found in the working-note contracts and
proofs. This conclusion is conditional on the stated assumptions. The difficult
semantic premises are exposed rather than established: faithful full-task
representation, source-semantic support and contradiction, and coverage of every
admissible action by a SOURCE-CONSISTENT proposed representative. The theorem
does not turn a finite hypothesis beam, a provenance string, or a zero ambiguity
score into open-domain certainty.

Soundness and risk checks

* The source semantics uses a nonempty coherent model class. A sound negative
  conclusion on a necessary claim cannot refute a source-consistent hypothesis.
* Positive support proves the winner's required source claims. Unique admissible
  query resolution is a separate conclusion and needs valid-class coverage plus
  elimination of every proposed rival, not merely one representative per class.
* A proposed two-version answer is joint. Treating the requested versions as
  mutually exclusive candidate answers violates the stated task contract.
* All automatically activated positive and negative rules must be included.
  Conflict blocking is global under the existing contract, and may make
  answerability and survivor elimination nonmonotone for fallible supplied maps.
* The error decomposition bounds error among all tasks. The selective bound
  correctly divides by the answer probability. An always-abstain policy does
  not acquire a meaningful zero selective error rate.
* The adaptive union bound requires an error bound at EVERY reachable history
  for the next issued judgment; average static benchmark accuracy is insufficient.
  The expected-call-count version needs the stated bounded predictable issuance.
* Conditional completeness explicitly requires expressible witnesses, their
  jointly affordable union, and complete search. Semantic uniqueness alone does
  not supply any of these.

Algorithm checks

* Both directions of every activation/OR linearization are necessary. Otherwise
  an optimizer could hide adverse activated judgments. The note includes both.
* For a general incompatibility graph, excluding only neighbors of the chosen
  winner is insufficient; two other surviving vertices can conflict. The graph
  variant correctly applies pair constraints to all survivors.
* Exact native rendered cost need not be additive or monotone. The oracle search
  uses only logically safe conflict/obligation pruning and proved cost bounds;
  it does not prune a partial set merely because its current rendered cost is
  over budget. Descendants of a feasible set may still be cheaper.
* The set-cover hardness reduction is cost-preserving for incremental costs and
  is correctly identified as inheritance of a classical result.
* Two-premise complementarity invalidates a blanket submodularity claim.
  Fixed k-lookahead's no-ratio example needs k+1 conjunctive actions and applies
  to the precisely restricted positive-improvement policy class, not every
  policy that happens to acquire one action at a time.
* The conditional approximation requires a globally conflict-free action pool,
  complete unique-minimal activation sets, a nonnegative union-resource cost,
  and a fixed available map. These sufficient conditions are deliberately
  stronger than checking the final selected set.
* The macro-cover proof compares FULL macro costs with primitive UNION costs.
  A resource is charged at most rho times in a constructed cover of the primitive
  optimum. The harmonic greedy argument and the independent m-bound then follow.
  Changing the greedy density to overlap-discounted marginal costs would require
  a different proof.
* A cost approximation is not a budget-preserving feasibility guarantee: even
  if OPT <= B, the approximate result may exceed B and must be rejected by the
  authorization gate. Additional capacity constraints are outside that proof.
* A fully observed-map optimum is a packing optimum or an oracle diagnostic.
  It is not an online acquisition policy with free access to unread outcomes.

Precision corrections requested during review

1. Core indistinguishability example: state explicitly that the decision uses
   only the identical transcript. A procedure with an additional source/query
   inspection channel need not be observationally identical.
2. Core failure-localization theorem: retain the coherent nonempty semantic
   model assumption explicitly, rather than only by the earlier setup.
3. Algorithm rho: give a convention for no positive-cost resources. The author
   adopted rho=1; every incremental cost is then zero.
4. Algorithm fixed-lookahead proof: the broad policy definition only ensures
   the chosen move CONTAINS costly d, not that it chooses d alone. The author
   changed the cost conclusion to at least M; the unbounded ratio remains.
5. Global approximation scope: applying the procedure to only targets passing
   its conditions gives a bound against that target subset. A bound against
   the global optimum needs the assumptions on its feasible target runs.
6. Side budgets: the author explicitly excludes additional hard side constraints
   from the classical cover bound. Final native-token capacity still needs a
   checked feasible certificate, whatever the acquisition-cost objective.
7. Risk theorem protocol premise: every emitted answer must pass the finite
   MECHANICAL gate and retain its chosen support/refutation proofs. Exact
   representations alone do not force an arbitrary emitter to apply that gate.
   This premise is now explicit in the theorem/setup; it does not assume that
   the semantic judgments or coverage assertion are true.
8. Initial evidence in the unique-activation lemma: the sole-provider union
   need cover only the remaining uncovered premise atoms, equivalently the
   union with E0 must cover the full premise. Both notes now say so explicitly.
9. Approximation versus authorization: the returned set meets the structural
   constraints. Coverage and actual budget still need to pass; even the primary
   cost approximation need not fit B when OPT fits B.
10. Online conflict handling: adding a new signed judgment cannot remove an
    existing positive/negative conflict from a monotone signed ledger. The
    policy now abstains; any supersession/adjudication needs a separate justified
    versioned protocol preserving original judgments, outside this guarantee.
11. Solver diagnostic mismatch: the first code draft excluded a candidate on
    any negative sign even when that claim also had a positive sign. The frozen
    contract makes that claim unresolved and can restore the survivor. This
    was corrected to refutation only by an uncontested negative and covered by
    a targeted test. Global conflict blocking was present before and after;
    this defect did not authorize a conflicting answer.
12. Control-runner robustness: external fixture receipt paths and rejection of
    unknown cost modes were corrected and independently checked.

Authored finite mathematical check

The reviewer independently exhaustively checked the unique-minimal-activation
criterion on 4,056 finite incidence maps with 1--4 independent actions, 1--3
required uncovered atoms, and every atom having at least one provider. For each
map, all action subsets were enumerated and inclusion-minimal activating sets
were compared with the union of sole providers. There were zero discrepancies:
one unique minimal activating set existed exactly when the sole-provider union
covered every required atom, and that set equaled the union. This finite check
supports implementation of the condition; the mathematical argument in the
note remains the proof. It is not natural-data validation, a model evaluation,
or evidence that real source actions satisfy this restrictive condition.

Reproduction of this small mathematical check (Python standard library):

  from itertools import product
  cases = 0
  for n in range(1, 5):
      for m in range(1, 4):
          for providers in product(range(1, 1 << n), repeat=m):
              feasible = [x for x in range(1 << n)
                          if all(x & p for p in providers)]
              minimal = [x for x in feasible
                         if not any(y != x and (y & x) == y for y in feasible)]
              mandatory = 0
              for p in providers:
                  if p & (p - 1) == 0:
                      mandatory |= p
              criterion = all(mandatory & p for p in providers)
              assert criterion == (len(minimal) == 1)
              if criterion:
                  assert minimal[0] == mandatory
              cases += 1
  assert cases == 4056

Publication and novelty boundary

These results are a rigorous formulation and a conditional analysis of an
evidence-certificate method. Boolean encoding, exhaustive checking, union bounds,
weighted set cover, and its logarithmic limits are standard machinery. This
review does not certify a fundamentally new mathematical theorem or superiority
over previous RAG methods. Any claimed methodological contribution still needs
an accurate prior-art audit and controlled local empirical evidence with strong
structure-aware and sufficiency-driven comparators.
