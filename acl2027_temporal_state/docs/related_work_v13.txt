Bounded literature and novelty check v0.13
Checked 3 October 2026; four search queries; primary-source comparison

Decision

No novel contribution is established by ordinary versus paired-version BM25.
The ongoing two-history experiment is a development diagnostic. Existing work
already studies version filtering, explicit and implicit document changes,
outdated evidence, temporal table selection, and decomposition of retrieval
and reasoning. Adding version nodes, retrieving both versions, or separating
retrieval from generation errors cannot by itself support an ACL novelty claim.

This audit is separate from the frozen four-PDF experiment corpus. It adds no
source histories, questions, reference answers, predictions or experiments.
It is a bounded selection of nearby work, not an exhaustive review or a
certification of the literature available at an eventual ACL 2027 deadline.
Publication dates below are from the primary records, not search crawl dates.

Verified nearby work

1. Huwiler, Stockinger and Fuerst: VersionRAG (arXiv v1, 9 October 2025).
   https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08109v1
   Sections 3-5 explicitly organize documents, versions, content and changes
   in a graph. Query classification selects content, version or change
   retrieval, with version filtering and indexed differences between states.
   VersionQA contains 100 manually curated questions over 34 technical files.
   The paper compares a top-five dense retriever and graph retrieval under
   several readers; its evaluation is therefore more than a simple latest-
   document selector. Section 6.5 separately varies indexing and retrieval
   models. The paper's scope is discrete versions; its limitations acknowledge
   gaps in calendar-time reasoning and reliance on extracted version metadata.
   Project implication (our inference): any later method must be compared
   against version-filtered retrieval and an indexed-change comparator.
   A new implementation of these ingredients is not a new research problem.

2. Ouyang et al.: HoH (ACL, July 2025).
   https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.301.pdf
   Sections 3-5 construct questions and old/current evidence from Wikipedia
   snapshots, then analyze retrieval and generation separately. The pipeline
   filters textual differences and uses a trained factual-change classifier
   before question generation. Its experiments include current, outdated and
   distractor evidence, passage ordering, and temporal retrieval comparators.
   Appendix E extends checks to a stronger proprietary reader on a subset.
   Project implication (our inference): showing that old text confuses a small
   model, or that both-version evidence improves some answers, repeats an
   established concern. A later study needs matched evidence budgets, passage-
   order controls and retrieval coverage measures, not just answer accuracy.

3. Cao et al.: Re3 (ACL, July 2026).
   https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1180.pdf
   Section 3 combines temporal relevance encoding with fact-grouped recency
   filtering. Its assumptions distinguish content time from publication time:
   publication time is available, and the latest authoritative record for a
   fact is taken as current truth. False latest records and source-reliability
   disputes are outside that setting. Section 4 compares retrieval and answer
   performance, including a cross-encoder reranker and several generators.
   Project implication (our inference): relevance plus recency is already a
   substantive comparator. Our unknown first-public times must not be filled
   in to fit its assumptions. A correction of a historical estimate and a
   new estimate over a changed population need not have the same semantics,
   even when document version order is known.

4. Shankarampeta et al.: TRANSIENTTABLES (NAACL, April 2025).
   https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.332.pdf
   The dataset has 3,971 questions over 14,133 tables spanning 1,238 entities.
   Section 2 distinguishes explicit and implicit temporal questions and
   includes table selection, ratios, percentages and comparisons. Section 3
   decomposes answering into temporal grounding, attribute selection and
   analytical reasoning, with different context granularities. Its examples
   concern changing semi-structured values rather than establishing a
   correction certificate for every document revision.
   Project implication (our inference): separating table extraction, units,
   temporal selection and numerical reasoning is necessary diagnostic hygiene,
   but that decomposition alone is not novel. Any finance-table extension
   should preserve headers, row labels, units and the relevant reporting scope.

Unresolved current lead

The searches also surfaced Sobhani et al., Time Present and Time Past:
Benchmarking Large Language Models on Temporally Evolving Document
Understanding, arXiv:2608.08512, with a search-record date of 9 August 2026.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08512
The abstract-page, HTML-v1 and PDF opens all returned tool-level internal
errors. The search record describes version resolution over amended official
texts, so this is a material novelty lead. Its full methods and claims were
not verified; it is omitted from the verified bibliography and is not used
to assert a gap. No further search queries were issued after the four-query
cap. Access failure does not invalidate the paper or establish its absence.

Potential research question, still unproven

The defensible candidate is narrower than version-aware RAG: can a reader
decide whether two reported results are comparable before stating that one
corrects, replaces or contradicts the other? A possible representation would
attach evidence for population, inclusion criteria, measurement definition,
units, reporting interval and authority to each result. It would distinguish
an explicitly corrected same-scope result, a revised analysis with changed
scope, a retained result, and insufficient evidence. Unknown fields remain
unknown; newer document order alone does not determine these relations.

This is our proposed hypothesis, not a capability proven absent from the
four papers, an implemented algorithm, or a claimed first. In particular,
VersionRAG could retrieve the needed change evidence, and a competent reader
could make the distinction without any extra machinery. The fixed experiment
must first establish whether such a residual failure exists.

Conditions for further investment

- Separate missing evidence, damaged table extraction, wrong units, incorrect
  reading and unsupported scope attribution. Do not call all of them temporal
  reasoning errors. Inspect citations and actual retrieved content.
- Keep the existing simple BM25 comparison as a diagnostic. A later study
  would need matched version filtering, dense/hybrid retrieval with reranking,
  a change-index comparator, and a competent reader. Do not call an adapted
  approximation an executed reproduction of VersionRAG or Re3.
- If the error persists with adequate evidence and a competent reader, design
  a separate blinded evaluation across independently held-out document
  histories. Review scope labels and abstentions before predictions; measure
  both correct comparisons and false claims of comparability. Include a
  scope-metadata ablation with matched context budget.
- If ordinary reading works, or errors disappear after extraction and basic
  competence are repaired, this diagnostic supplies no new method claim.
  Neither a positive nor a negative result on two familiar histories can
  establish prevalence, broad gains, or readiness for ACL 2027 submission.
