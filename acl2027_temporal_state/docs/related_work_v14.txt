Evidence retrieval and comparison: primary-source audit v0.14
Checked 3 October 2026; six local search queries plus two supplied primary links

Research decision

Treat improved structural retrieval and a competent reader as the next
baseline, not as a newly established contribution. The nearest additional
work already retrieves missing sides of financial comparisons, predicts
structured evidence gaps, selects verbatim evidence and judges sufficiency
before answering. A graph, missing-slot controller or header-preserving
chunker by itself would not distinguish this project.

The candidate worth testing is more specific: retrieving evidence that
distinguishes competing interpretations of the quantities being compared,
while keeping every selected quantity attached to its actual source scope.
The operative contrast is not merely whether both numbers were retrieved,
but whether the available evidence warrants the requested comparison. This
is a falsifiable proposal; novelty and empirical benefit remain unestablished.

The two v0.13 literature notes were read first. Their version-aware,
scope-governance, table-structure and TIDE findings still apply. They are
not rewritten here. This pass adds six inspected primary manuscripts;
one closer-looking lead remains inaccessible. No evaluation corpus, question,
reference answer or native output was added or modified by this lookup.

Nearest additional threats

1. Choe, Kim and Jung, HiREC, arXiv v3 dated 6 November 2025.
   https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20368v3
   Section 4 retrieves documents using distinguishing company/period
   information, then reranks passages with a table-adapted model. It filters
   evidence, checks answerability and generates complementary queries for
   missing information. Cross-company and cross-period numeric comparisons
   are explicit motivation. This directly covers the idea of retrieving
   a missing side of a comparison. Required comparator: a hierarchy plus
   generic answerability/complementary-query loop under our reader and
   resource limits. An adaptation must be labeled as such, not as a faithful
   reproduction unless its actual released procedure is reproduced.

2. Li et al., S2G-RAG, ACL, July 2026.
   https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1185.pdf
   Section 3 predicts sufficiency and structured gaps with target, slot,
   category and description fields. Gaps drive later queries. A pointer-only
   extractor retains original sentences, including disambiguating context.
   The judge is trained from execution traces and separated from generation.
   Thus structured missing slots, iterative stopping and source pointers
   are already mechanisms, not an open novelty claim. Required comparator:
   generic gap-directed retrieval with the same evidence representation,
   reader and call/token limits as the proposed specialized controller.

3. Chen, Hua and Xiao, RINSE, arXiv v1 dated 27 September 2026.
   https://arxiv.org/html/2609.37469v1
   Sections 3-4 judge question/evidence sufficiency without generating an
   answer and combine local question coverage, answer-span and cross-passage
   signals. Their paired tests audit shortcuts from passage count, lexical
   overlap, question wording and evidence order. This covers pre-generation
   sufficiency and cautions against easy missing-header negatives. Required
   comparator: a general sufficiency scorer or adequately pinned context-only
   judge. Any new attachment test must control obvious surface cues and
   separately report naturally retrieved failures and authored stress cases.

4. Wang, Wang and Chen, FinLongDocQA/FinLongDocAgent, arXiv v1,
   4 April 2026. https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03664v1
   Sections 3-4 preserve report pages and use expansion, solving and evaluation
   roles to collect cross-table numerical components. Evaluation checks
   missing components and account matches, then feeds them into retrieval.
   The introduction identifies value extraction, formulas and mixed units
   as distinct difficulties. This blocks a novelty claim for financial
   component completion or retrieval-plus-verification alone. Our narrower
   target must concern warranted cross-version attribution, beyond merely
   finding operands and computing an answer.

5. Kim et al., Tree of Clarifications (ToC), EMNLP, December 2023.
   https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.63.pdf
   Section 3 recursively generates disambiguated question/answer nodes,
   reranks passages per query and prunes nodes against the original
   question's scope. It then answers across valid interpretations. Scope
   checking, multiple interpretations and retrieval-guided clarification
   therefore are prior work. Its inspected objective covers valid question
   interpretations; our proposed acquisition target separates incompatible
   source attachments for a specified comparison. This distinction needs
   testing against budget-matched enumeration, reranking and pruning, with
   identical initial candidate hypotheses. Renaming ambiguity is insufficient.

6. Gao et al., REFUEL, ACL-IJCNLP, August 2021.
   https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.253.pdf
   Section 2 fuses retrieved evidence to predict answers and disambiguated
   questions. Round-trip prediction discovers missed interpretations using
   the same retrieved passage pool; conditional answer probabilities filter
   candidates. Answer partitions and iterative disambiguation are thus
   established. A possible distinction is selecting new source witnesses to
   reject wrong attachments, rather than expanding valid interpretations
   over an existing pool. Test this by holding the candidate pool and
   hypothesis generation fixed and ablating witness acquisition separately.

Proposed mechanism to test, not an implemented or validated method

1. Build the conventional structural baseline first. A source unit retains
   its table/section identity, row and column labels, unit/scale, period,
   footnotes and explicit continuation links. Every attachment points to
   actual source spans. Ambiguous attachments remain unresolved. This
   structure is an input requirement and baseline feature, not our novelty.

2. Parse the requested comparison into explicit obligations. Depending on
   the question, these can include two reported quantities, their reporting
   versions, metric definitions, target periods, population/inclusion rules
   or an explicit correction statement. Do not demand every conceivable
   field for every question. The parser is fallible and must be evaluated;
   reference obligations and gold scope labels cannot enter inference.

3. Maintain candidate interpretations, not a single guessed binding. For
   example, a value can refer to either a prior-year column or a restatement
   column; a percentage can use a changed denominator. Label each required
   binding as supported, contradicted or unresolved, with source pointers.
   A descriptive report of two differently scoped results can be legitimate;
   it must not become an unsupported assertion that they estimate the same
   thing or that their difference measures the effect of a correction.

4. Retrieve a distinguishing witness for the highest-priority unresolved
   binding: the actual column heading, unit exception, population statement,
   continuation caption or revision explanation. Select actions by expected
   reduction of unresolved comparison obligations per charged token/call,
   using a predeclared policy. A cheap first implementation can use fixed
   typed priorities; do not call this optimal information gain. Generic
   missing-slot retrieval must have the same opportunity to fetch these spans.

5. Pack whole required evidence bundles under the shared input cap. A number
   whose interpretation depends on a header cannot retain the number while
   dropping that header merely to fit. Charge every copied source word,
   label and instruction. When a bundle does not fit, record the exclusion
   or return a scoped partial answer/abstention; no invisible free metadata.
   These records are auditable support judgments, not formal correctness
   proofs. An extraction mistake can still invalidate the whole comparison.

The specific hypothesis is that choosing evidence to resolve competing
bindings, rather than choosing evidence only for topical relevance or generic
answerability, lowers unsupported comparison claims at matched resource
limits without losing supported answers. All components resemble existing
work; only a demonstrated residual benefit and a precise analysis could
justify a distinct contribution. A null result remains a useful decision.

Minimum discriminating comparisons and ablations

A. Structural baseline: hybrid retrieval and reranking with source-faithful
   table/section context and ordinary version/metadata filters. This tests
   whether conventional retrieval already resolves the observed failure.
B. Generic active baseline: the same representation with a HiREC- or
   S2G-style gap loop and a general sufficiency judge. Hold reader, query
   access, maximum turns and source-selection permissions fixed.
C. Remove comparison-specific obligations while retaining closure and active
   retrieval. This isolates specialization from better structure or more work.
D. Keep the obligation state but choose next evidence by relevance or generic
   gaps instead of a distinguishing-binding policy. This tests the proposed
   action selection itself.
E. Keep retrieval fixed and compare complete support bundles with ordinary
   packing under the same token cap. Report displaced facts and attachment
   mistakes; do not add gold headers only to the proposed condition.
F. Separate version membership, unit/period attachment and population/metric
   compatibility. Ordinary version filtering may solve one without solving
   the others. Evaluate their interactions rather than renaming all failures.
G. Cross reader and representation factors on the development diagnostic:
   old/new retrieval under the same stronger reader, and old/new reader on
   the same contexts. This prevents simultaneous upgrades from disguising
   which change helped. The old eight questions remain development-only.
H. Add an interpretation-enumeration comparator inspired by ToC/REFUEL, with
   the same initial hypotheses, source candidates and verifier budget. Keep
   its scope pruning and evidence reranking; vary only whether the selector
   prioritizes separation of incompatible source bindings. Check candidate
   proposal recall independently so better hypotheses cannot masquerade as
   better evidence selection. These are controlled adaptations, not faithful
   reproductions unless all original procedures are actually implemented.

Evaluation and stopping rule

Use a pinned reader with independently checked competence on complete tables
and scope distinctions. All methods share the maximum final-input budget;
report actual token use, cumulative retrieval/controller/generator costs and
latency separately. Iterative calls are not free. Include a larger-context
structural baseline on a clearly labeled cost/performance curve so a tight
budget does not manufacture a win.

Review new held-out histories and comparison references before predictions,
with independent annotation where feasible and an explicit unknown outcome.
Score numerical sign/scale, source-attachment correctness, relation to the
queried scope, citation support and abstention separately. The main target
should combine supported-answer coverage with the rate of false comparison
claims; withholding all answers is not success. If using constructed
attachment substitutions, preserve or balance length, entity names, numbers
and lexical overlap, and report those tests separately from natural cases.

Stop the method claim if conventional structural retrieval plus a competent
reader works, if a general gap controller matches the specialized one, if
gains disappear when tokens/calls are charged, or if only reference-supplied
scope/attachments make the method work. Also stop if real histories cannot
support reliable comparison labels. Never select the proposed mechanism
because it happens to fix the two already-known histories.

This audit recommends an experiment, not ACL 2027 acceptance, publishability,
novelty certification, or positive results. No compared method was executed.
The machine-readable search trail and bibliography are adjacent v14 artifacts.
