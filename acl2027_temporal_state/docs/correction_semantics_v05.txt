Correction-aware support replacement, version 0.5
================================================

Purpose and evidence status

This checkpoint implements conditional support replacement before the existing
temporal-network materializer. It is an engineering/reference semantics for
selected interpretations, not a learned correction detector or an algorithmic
novelty claim. All decoder methods must receive the same candidate graph,
scores, correction policy, support dependencies, and state-semantics hook.
The reference implementation does not inspect questions or evaluation labels.

A correction changes which earlier assertion support is usable at a later
information prefix. It does not terminate that assertion's real-world event
interval on the correction's date. Original assertions and selected links are
preserved in an audit ledger; inactive assertions supply no temporal variables,
observations, endpoint bounds, or factual answer support. The original earlier
prefix must be materialized separately from its own eligible source set.

Immutable policy contract

CorrectionPolicy has version='0.5', source_authorities, correction_evidence,
and support_dependencies. All collections are tuples of frozen typed records.
The scored-cache wrapper binds policy content and provenance cryptographically
along with the original v0.4 candidate/score cache. Hashes certify artifact
consistency, not truth or historical availability.

SourceAuthority(source_id, authority_id, evidence_spans) attributes the assertion
originator in a source. The authority is the issuer of the statement, which can
differ from its hosting provider: an issuer filing on SEC is not thereby a
statement issued by SEC. Evidence consists of exact nonempty Unicode-codepoint
source slices. One authority attribution per source is supported. Sources that
mix multiple speakers or quotation authorities require preprocessing into
separate attribution units or a richer future claim-level authority schema.
Do not equate a web domain with correction authority.

CorrectionEvidence(link_id, reference_span, action='replace',
coverage='whole_assertion') certifies the extracted interpretation of one
existing CORRECTS candidate. Its reference span must match the link's source
offsets and actual correcting-source text. The selected link names the exact
replacement reading and exact target reading; authority attribution is required
for both source records, with equal authority IDs. These checks verify structure
and source-slice integrity. They do not independently prove that the text entails
the attribution, reference resolution, or full replacement. Those remain
annotated/model-selected assumptions and must be assessed separately.

The only supported action is replacement of a whole atomic assertion. Partial
field edits and bare retractions are rejected. Never represent a retraction of
support as a negative real-world occupancy interval. TemporalClaim remains
unchanged; its replacement factual content uses ASSERT or UNRESOLVED operation,
and the selected CORRECTS link carries the correction relation. CORRECTS as a
standalone TemporalClaim.operation remains unsupported by the bounded module.

SupportDependency(dependent_claim_id, prerequisite_claim_id, evidence_spans)
declares an essential factual support prerequisite. Its evidence must be an
exact slice of the dependent's own source. Multiple prerequisite edges are
conjunctive: every declared prerequisite is necessary, not interchangeable.
The complete candidate dependency graph must be acyclic, and a prerequisite
source cannot be dated later than its dependent source. This is a factual
support dependence, not a dependency of the correction notice's speech act.

Structural errors such as unknown IDs, duplicate records, malformed spans,
unsupported actions, or essential-support cycles are rejected when the policy
is validated. A CORRECTS candidate may lack a certificate or authority record;
that candidate is infeasible if selected, while its null alternative remains
available. The policy does not manufacture a correction for an unresolved cue.
Every candidate claim must also remain individually well-formed before selection.
The projection can remove conflicts between valid claims, but cannot ingest an
intrinsically impossible resolved interval and repair its schema through a later
correction. Such source errors need an unresolved/raw-record representation or a
future richer malformed-assertion type.

Selected-state algorithm

1. Validate exactly one selected reading and one link or null for each mention.
   Every non-null link must bind the selected resolved endpoint readings and
   preserve the normalized factual key, including exact scope. A correction
   cannot spread to other relations, scopes, neighboring metrics, or histories.
   RESTATES also preserves value and polarity. CHANGES requires distinct
   positive values. Current identity normalization is the existing bounded
   normalization, not approximate scope matching.

2. Validate each selected correction's explicit reference and common asserting
   authority. Its target source must be earlier than or equal in day-level
   availability to the correcting source. A reference is required on different
   days as well as the same day: chronology alone is not correction evidence.
   Reject cycles in the selected correction-reference graph.

3. Mark every explicitly targeted assertion directly withdrawn. This set is
   monotone within the selected prefix. For A <- B <- C, both A and B remain
   withdrawn and C is the active replacement. Suppression of B's replacement
   assertion does not undo B's original correction notice. Reinstating A's value
   requires an explicit new assertion C with that value; the old A is not revived.
   Retraction of a correction notice, cancellation of a withdrawal, and arbitrary
   belief-revision semantics are outside this checkpoint.

4. Propagate essential-support inactivity to a fixed point. If any prerequisite
   is not selected as that exact candidate claim, or is inactive, mark the
   dependent inactive. Record missing_prerequisite or inactive_prerequisite
   separately from direct replacement. Missing support does not reject the whole
   reading vector: all-null link assignments must remain available to each
   decoder's reading-selection baseline. No undeclared prerequisite is inferred.

5. Remove inactive claims and their incident RESTATES/CHANGES edges from the
   temporal graph. Materialize the surviving claims with the existing exact
   integer difference-constraint solver, preserving every surviving claim's
   interval bounds, polarity, modality, observations, and report horizon.
   Correction edges contribute no event-date inequalities.

6. Answer over the active claims with the same bounded query semantics. A
   planned replacement remains a planned claim and does not prove realized
   outcomes. Two independently selected sibling corrections of the same target
   remain active. If their content is incompatible for the queried event time,
   the answer remains indeterminate; no latest-source or preferred-authority
   ranking silently selects a winner.

RESTATES and evidence dependence

RESTATES in v0.4 means a shared temporal episode. It does not mean a source copied
another source. Therefore a correction of A does not automatically withdraw R
just because R RESTATES A. R's edge to A is deactivated, preventing A's bounds
and observations from tightening R. R's own assertion survives unless an
explicit essential-support dependency makes it inactive. Its status is 'no
declared derivative dependence', not independently verified corroboration.

This distinction is essential. Blanket component deletion would suppress
independent evidence; leaving the original episode equality active would retain
withdrawn temporal evidence. Explicitly copied or derived assertions can be
withdrawn transitively through SupportDependency instead.

Source eligibility and same-day limitations

The materializer accepts only a strict eligible prefix: all sources, source
contexts, candidates, policy evidence, and dependency records must belong to
that prefix. An explicit same-day target reference gives direction to a
correction relation, but supplies no reliable clock time or within-day cutoff.
The implementation never invents such a time. Publication datelines, SEC
acceptance metadata, immutable raw-response captures, and demonstrated first
public availability are different evidential objects. Benchmark admission
must establish availability/version eligibility outside this materializer.

Provenance and the decoder interface

materialize_corrected_selection(problem, reading_ids, link_ids, claims, policy)
returns the existing CoupledMemory interface with active claims only. Its
correction_ledger retains all original selected claim records, all selected
links/readings, active/inactive IDs and reasons, operative notices, deactivated
temporal edges, authority declarations, and timing assumptions. Original records
are not modified.

Factual support and support-control provenance are separate concepts. Active
claims retain their own support sources and ordinary context dependencies.
Sources of operative correction notices, their target sources, and both
endpoints' declared source contexts are conservatively retained as control
dependencies. These justify the source-attribution and reference-resolution
decision, including the target's identification. Controls propagate through
inactive essential dependents, including across factual keys, and attach to
active competitors with each affected key. Thus removing a derived competitor
cannot silently omit the correction that enabled an answer. A source containing
a withdrawn replacement assertion may still be cited for its retained correction
notice in a chain or as a target-identification record. Its withdrawn assertion
contributes no fact or interval constraint. The broad same-key control-source
union can overcite records; it is not a minimal proof trace. The ledger separates
these control dependencies from active factual assertions.
When inactivity instead follows a missing selected prerequisite, controls also
retain the prerequisite's source, its mention context, the currently selected
alternative reading's context (including unresolved readings), and a resolved
alternative's claim context. Choosing an alternative identity can therefore
remove dependent support without hiding the source/context of that choice.

make_correction_state_semantics returns the shared decoder hook. It validates
the selected state, performs support projection, and returns only the active
RESTATES components, or None for an infeasible link assignment. It must replace
the legacy decoder's global exact-date checks, not run after them: withdrawn
assertions can otherwise cause premature RESTATES date-conflict rejection.
Local endpoint/key checks remain in the decoder. Scoring retains every selected
historical interpretation and link; inactive support is not equivalent to an
unselected candidate, and no method receives a special objective advantage.

Validation and scope of results

The local authored tests exercise atomic replacement, same-day references,
authority mismatch, scope isolation, chains, siblings, explicit reinstatement,
dependency cascades and missing prerequisites, cycles, interval deactivation,
query modality, exact provenance, and immutable historical records. A separate
finite oracle independently enumerates admissible active subsets. These are
implementation diagnostics. They do not establish extraction accuracy, calibrated
authority judgments, held-out QA gains, a joint-decoding advantage, or scalability.
