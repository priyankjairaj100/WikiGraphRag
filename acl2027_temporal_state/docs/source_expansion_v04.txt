SOURCE EXPANSION v0.4 — PRIMARY-SOURCE DISCOVERY
Recorded 2026-10-02. Companion: data/source_register/expansion_v04.json.

Outcome and status

Five additional histories and eleven substantive source records were inspected, covering five publishers and three broad families: service/browser lifecycle policy, mission planning, and corporate forecast correction. They do not repeat the five histories already in seed_sources.json. One pair is a genuine explicit correction: Lyft's issuer correction identifies an erroneous forecast magnitude, while its original SEC exhibit still preserves the earlier wording. Four other histories concern changed plans, not factual corrections.

Every record remains discovery-only. No raw historical response bodies were downloaded or hashed, no QA items were written, no train/dev/test split was assigned, and no method was run on these histories. The register includes exact URLs, reported dates and date bases, short evidence fragments, retrieval references, scope/time schemas, grouping rules, and unresolved admission gaps. Model-assisted discovery is not human adjudication.

1. Docker Free Team: a useful pre-reversal archive

Docker's March 24, 2023 announcement reverses its Free Team sunset policy. Its currently served March 16 post already contains the later reversal banner, so using that live text in a March 16 prefix would leak future information. The publisher links to an accessible March 22 Wayback capture. That prior version retains the planned private-repository suspension and distinctions among subscription tiers and public/private content.

The historical FAQ says private repositories would be suspended April 13. The reversal says existing subscribers no longer need to migrate by April 14. Keep these separate action/date statements: differing deadlines do not by themselves establish a typo or correction. The original March 14 email is acknowledged in the reversal but was not acquired. Archive timestamps provide a promising availability anchor, while response-level replay provenance and the reversal's historical version remain to be secured.

2. Chrome third-party cookies: changing proposals with scoped conditions

The selected sequence covers a conditional broad-restriction plan, a July 2024 user-choice proposal, and an April 2025 decision against a new standalone prompt. The pages distinguish ordinary browsing, Incognito defaults, partial-user testing, and regulator-dependent rollout. These are distinct scopes and modalities; a proposal is not operational availability.

The earlier countdown page has an October 2023 footer but includes retrospective January 2024 testing language and a later user-choice banner. That is direct evidence that its current body cannot safely reconstruct the October 2023 information state. Footer dates alone are inadequate version evidence. The selected sequence ends at April 2025 and is not intended as a complete account of later Privacy Sandbox policy.

3. Artemis II/III: schedule changes outside commercial products

NASA release 24-006, dated January 9, 2024, gives revised September 2025 and September 2026 targets for Artemis II and III, respectively. Release 24-145, dated December 5, gives April 2026 and mid-2027 targets. Neither release establishes the corresponding launch or landing as completed. The earlier page also carries a neighboring Artemis IV target, useful for testing whether an update incorrectly spreads to another mission.

4. PlayStation legacy commerce: reversal, later retirement, regional scope

Sony's April 19, 2021 post reverses the proposed PS3/Vita store closure while retaining the PSP commerce retirement. A separately inspected July 1, 2026 post announces region-dependent future closure windows and distinguishes new purchases from continued access to previously purchased content. These records make a useful multi-device, multi-region history without relying on a leadership relation.

The original pre-April-2021 notification was not acquired. The later post's acknowledgment of that plan cannot stand in for an early prefix. Nor do the two inspected announcements prove uninterrupted actual operation in the intervening years or prove that any announced regional closure occurred. A future test needs the missing original, pinned versions, and independently sourced outcomes where questions require actual state.

Methodological value: cancellation of one device's plan must not cancel another's; a new retirement years later is distinct from correcting the former continuation announcement. Regions and purchase/redownload capabilities are identity fields, not optional qualifiers.

5. Lyft: explicit correction with an independently preserved original

The original February 13, 2024 SEC exhibit reports FY2024 adjusted EBITDA margin expansion of approximately 500 basis points year over year. The issuer replacement explicitly identifies that earlier bullet and replaces the magnitude with 50. This is stronger than inferring correction from two inconsistent numbers: the source itself declares the replacement. It corrects the reported forecast; neither number is a realized financial result.

The SEC filing index identifies accession 0001759509-24-000011 and displays acceptance at 2024-02-13 16:06:27. The inspected correction page supplies a release date but does not establish exact public release time. Acceptance is not automatically first public availability, and the index does not state its timezone. Day-only timestamps cannot support a reliable cutoff between two same-day releases. No secondary-source minute estimates have been adopted.

Sampling and admission discipline

These five histories are a targeted convenience sample for feasibility. They cannot support a representativeness or generalization claim. Future expansion should predeclare publisher/date windows, enumerate every release matching fixed discovery terms, and log exclusions before observing model outputs. Include unchanged announcements, harmless restatements, concurrent scoped states, unresolved contradictions, actual outcomes diverging from plans, and ambiguous boundaries. Do not retain only cases where the joint method wins.

Keep each full history/program, related device or mission variants, reprints, translations and archived/live versions in one split group. This discovery set has already been inspected by development agents and is not a blind test. Freeze original bytes, capture metadata, extraction rules and hashes before creating admissible prefixes. Verify public availability separately from report date and target time. Retrieve original/correction versions independently; never reconstruct an earlier source by deleting a later banner without version evidence.

Recommended acquisition order: first pin the Lyft original/correction and resolve same-day timing; then secure Docker's already located prior capture and its reversal; then obtain NASA release versions. Chrome and PlayStation have richer scope but more substantial historical-version gaps. This order is based on feasibility and coverage, not observed method performance.
