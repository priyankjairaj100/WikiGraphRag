Inline XBRL source-linkage semantics review v14
Reviewed 2026-10-03. Specification-only, model-assisted shared-session development review; not independent human gold. No filing values, QA, model predictions, numeric normalization, or new report downloads were used for this semantics note. Checks below are recommendations, not completed conformance tests.

Minimal checks and interpretation limits

1. References and targets. Resolve contextRef/unitRef across the declared Inline XBRL Document Set; record missing/ambiguous IDs. Context/unit IDs are unique across that set. Preserve target/default partitioning separately; target mapping may replace IDs with s-equal resources. A successful single-file lookup establishes local linkage only. [IX 3.1–3.2, 10.1–10.2.2, 14.1.2]

2. Entity and time. Retain identifier scheme and content, instant/duration/forever, and original date lexemes. Date-only startDate begins its day; endDate/instant denotes midnight following its day. Concept periodType compatibility requires taxonomy information. Context IDs are references, not cross-history identities. Preserve non-dimensional segment/scenario content. [BASE 4.7.2–4.7.4, 4.10]

3. Dimensions. Preserve expanded dimension/member QNames and segment versus scenario placement. Typed members contain schema-constrained XML. Detect repeated dimensions. Omitted explicit dimensions can inherit DTS defaults; defaults must not be explicitly reported. Without the DTS, default/domain/hypercube validity and typed-content interpretation remain unresolved. Missing dimensions alone do not establish consolidated scope. Dimensional equality differs from base context equality. [DIM 2.7, 3.1.4, 3.2]

4. Units. Preserve expanded measure QNames, multiplicity, and numerator/denominator structure. Unit IDs and display symbols do not establish unit equivalence. Simple products and divided units have distinct shapes; cancellation and concept-type restrictions need validation. [BASE 4.8.2–4.8.4, 4.10]

5. Numeric metadata. Retain format QName, sign, scale, nil, decimals/precision. Conversion order is transformation, power-of-ten scaling, then sign. Nil carries no numeric quantity. Nested nonFraction elements have constraints, including shared format/scale/unit properties; retain each occurrence. Registry namespace resolution does not establish transformation support. [IX 10.1–10.2.3, 16.4] Decimals/precision express accuracy; they are not display scaling. [BASE 4.6.3–4.6.5]

6. Visibility. Header/hidden facts require separate provenance. Hidden facts can support machine linkage without establishing visible-table evidence. DOM/CSS markers alone do not verify browser visibility. [IX 8.1.2, 9.1.2]

7. Repetition. Preserve occurrence anchors. Repeated concept/contextRef/unitRef strings are only candidates: semantic duplicate classification requires aspect equality and accuracy/value analysis. Complete, consistent-rounded, and inconsistent duplicates differ; Inline conversion can retain duplicates. Do not silently choose, merge, or sum occurrences. The duplicate note is guidance, with formal OIM definitions taking precedence. [DUP 3.2–3.5, 4–5, 6.4]

Practical conclusion (reviewer inference)
Exact bytes/hashes, namespace-aware DOM anchors, and explicit local reference cardinalities support a useful source-only inventory. They do not certify taxonomy validity, registry conversion, numerical equivalence, visual grounding, temporal comparability, or answer sufficiency. Keep these outcomes separate before considering a structured-reader baseline.
