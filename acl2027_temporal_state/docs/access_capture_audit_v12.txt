v0.12 source-access provenance audit

The eight frozen URLs were attempted once, in their frozen order, sequentially.
All eight curl processes completed successfully as transport operations. Six
responses were HTTP 200 and yielded nonempty parsed text: four PDFs and two
abstract HTML pages. Both JAMA URLs returned HTTP 403; those failed source-access
outcomes remain in the public manifest with their response hashes. Their extracted
files contain only a newline and correctly have mechanical_parse_success=false.
No model output or source admission is established by this audit.

All 23 recorded audit checks passed. They bind the protocol, execution authorization,
admission protocol and referenced parents, collector and unchanged v08 helper,
extractor executable hashes, external frozen snapshots, source order, timestamps,
recorded commands, and every raw/text/header hash and size. The protocol, admission
design, collector freeze and authorization all precede retrieval. Both packaged
and external final manifests agree. All eight private effective URLs equal the
requested URLs, with zero redirects and no query or fragment to suppress.

Recorded command guards are HTTPS-only redirects, five maximum redirects, ten
seconds to connect, thirty seconds to transfer, 8 MiB per response, and curl -q to
disable implicit configuration. No retry, credentials, cookie or config arguments
appear. The largest payload is 4,876,961 bytes; all reported transfer times are
below one second. Across eight URLs there are 10,456,211 raw response bytes and
1,803,191 extracted-text bytes. The complete sequential capture including parsing
lasted 4.655882 seconds according to the recorded timestamps.

The public record fields match the metadata allowlist. Raw transfer JSON, effective
URL details, stderr, full headers, cookies and source text remain external. An
exact-hash scan of known current/prior external response, header and document files
against visible repository files found no copied source-bearing payload. This
check excludes trivial files at most 32 bytes and cannot establish the absence of
arbitrarily transformed copies. Detailed scan counts are in the JSON audit.

The six focused offline collector tests pass again, and the no-network frozen-input
verifier succeeds. No new network calls or model predictions were made for this
audit. Frozen inputs and results were not edited.

Audit limitations: this audit was performed by the collector's author, so it is
not independent implementation review or human gold. Separate source-semantic
reviewers assess identity, complete page coverage, versions and table fidelity.
Recorded receipts establish the visible attempts; they cannot externally attest
to absence of unlogged activity. No expected title was frozen, so lexical identity
checking is explicitly inapplicable. Transport completion, HTTP success, mechanical
parsing, heuristic usability and benchmark admission remain distinct statuses.
