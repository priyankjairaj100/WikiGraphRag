Source-grounded answer certificates: prior-art and theorem-transfer audit v21
Checked 2026-10-05. Targeted primary-source audit; not an exhaustive novelty certification.

Decision

The current combination is not enough, by itself, to establish a novel theory
paper. Cost-sensitive discrimination, answer equivalence classes, consistent
minimal explanations, conjunctions of evidence requirements, gap-driven retrieval,
and selective answering all have close precedents. A defensible narrower result
is a precise source-grounded joint-answer certificate contract, its explicit
coverage and error boundary, and an exact optimization formulation whose inputs,
costs and limited assurance are auditable. Those results can be useful without
being new general combinatorial or statistical theory. The manuscript must say
which propositions are elementary consequences, which are reductions to known
problems, and which additional result, if any, remains technically novel.

The possible empirical contribution is separate: automatically discovering and
acquiring relational source evidence that resolves residual scoped-answer
ambiguity under matched total costs. Neither a finite supplied-judgment solver
nor a constructed counterexample establishes that empirical contribution.

Theorem transfer: precise conditions and prohibited shortcuts

1. Fixed-answer set cover is not automatically full test cover.
   If each supplied evidence item individually excludes a known subset of rivals
   to a fixed target answer, covering all rivals is ordinary weighted set cover.
   If tests must separate every relevant pair, the pair-universe formulation is
   test cover. If the target answer is also selected, there is an additional
   choice among target-specific feasible certificates. This classification does
   not establish an approximation for the full proposed problem.

2. Conjunctions invalidate the obvious singleton coverage guarantee.
   Let one rival be contradicted only by the joint evidence {a,b}. Its exclusion
   score is 0 on the empty set and both singletons, and 1 on {a,b}. The marginal
   gain of b increases after seeing a, violating ordinary submodularity. This is
   a counterexample for that score, not a proof that no alternative surrogate or
   richer adaptive model can work. Two-step lookahead remains a heuristic unless
   a separate approximation proof is supplied.

3. Compiling bundles does not make their cost additive.
   Fixed, fully specified bundles can create an ordinary coverage objective if
   any one complete selected bundle independently settles an obligation. With
   shared spans, however, union-token cost differs from the sum of bundle costs.
   Any claimed guarantee must use the actual cost constraint. Construction of
   bundles, all source reads, proposal generation, and verifier invocations are
   not free preprocessing for an end-to-end claim.

4. EC2 is stronger prior art than a simplistic independence description suggests.
   It fixes finite hypotheses/tests/outcomes, a known joint prior, and test costs.
   Complete outcome realizations make tests deterministic; latent expansion can
   encode correlated noise. Thus unknown outcomes or correlated observations
   alone do not defeat its applicability. The needed missing assumptions here
   are a justified complete modeled universe, correct likelihood/prior or exact
   outcome semantics, and the requisite objective properties. An arbitrary
   symmetric incompatibility graph is not automatically an equivalence-class
   partition: compatibility must be transitive for that direct reduction.

5. Do not import the published EC2 constant without checking the later correction.
   The 2010 paper's Theorem 3 states (2 ln(1/p_min)+1) times optimal expected
   cost for rational priors and invokes the original adaptive-cover theorem.
   Golovin--Krause arXiv:1003.3967v5 (2017), Theorem 13 and its historical note,
   correct the earlier general expected-cover proof; the corrected statement
   uses stronger assumptions and a squared logarithm. The EC2 constant is
   recorded here as a published claim, not independently revalidated. This audit
   does not claim that EC2 itself has been disproved. Later general and
   independent-item cover results have different assumptions/parameters; no
   identical-constant repair is asserted here. The current proposal should use
   no transferred EC2 approximation factor.

6. ECED does not cover arbitrary correlated source/verifier errors.
   Its model has finite root causes with known prior/likelihoods, a target map,
   conditionally independent tests given the root cause, persistent outcomes,
   and unit test costs. Its theorem further uses binary tests and per-test noise
   rates independent of the root cause. It compares against an optimal policy
   attaining a smaller expected error, measured by that policy's worst-case
   number of tests. This is not a same-error expected-cost approximation. With
   positive flip probabilities, likelihood discounting is not logical falsity.

7. Adaptive maximization and adaptive minimum-cost cover are different theorems.
   Adaptive submodularity concerns conditional expected marginal benefit; it
   cannot be inferred from a static set score or from a greedy implementation.
   Budgeted maximization's familiar 1-1/e factor requires its own monotonicity,
   diminishing-return and budget assumptions. Cover additionally needs a goal,
   attainability and a gap below the goal. Self-certification is only over all
   consistent modeled realizations; it cannot certify that the real source
   universe or hypothesis list has been exhausted.

8. A source certificate requires assumptions beyond a logical solver.
   State the finite world/answer universe, the complete joint answer map, and
   the source-relative judgment semantics. Contradicted must mean positively
   refuted by acquired source premises; absent/not found/unverified is unknown.
   A supported answer needs its own complete source witness. Conflicting
   judgments must block authorization or be represented by an explicitly proved
   conflict semantics. A true hypothesis must not be deleted by an unsound
   verifier. Proofs conditional on correct supplied judgments do not validate a
   learned verifier, parser, candidate generator, or the truth of the source.

9. Joint answers need joint consistency.
   Independently supported slot values do not establish the compatibility of
   their joint scope/version/comparison relation. If each marginal was supported
   in a different possible interpretation, a product of those marginals can
   fabricate an impossible complete answer. Certificates and exclusion tests
   must range over consistent joint interpretations and the question's actual
   claims, including the relation asserted between quantities.

10. Candidate discovery and certificate optimization have different information.
    Cheapest evidence after all outcomes are known is an offline certificate
    problem. A policy paying to discover outcomes is an online/adaptive problem.
    Comparing the latter with an offline optimum is a lower-bound diagnostic,
    not a proof of optimal acquisition. Exact optimization over a supplied
    evidence/judgment table must be labeled annotation-conditioned. An unseen
    rival consistent with everything acquired can change the authorized answer;
    an unknown flag or finite beam cannot by itself assign that event a small
    probability. A calibrated coverage assumption needs independent evidence.

11. Selective-risk statements must condition correctly.
    If the probability of candidate omission is at most delta and the union of
    other assurance failures has probability at most epsilon, a conditional
    correctness proof implies P(wrong AND answer) <= delta+epsilon. It does not
    imply P(wrong | answer) <= delta+epsilon. If answer coverage is alpha>0, the
    immediate bound is min(1,(delta+epsilon)/alpha). Alternatively estimate the
    failure rates conditional on answering directly. Adaptive repeated verifier
    use requires simultaneous validity, a justified conditional guarantee, or
    an explicit error budget; a single-call validation rate is not a uniform
    run-level bound. No independence assumption is needed for the union bound.

12. Use minimal and minimum accurately.
    An inclusion-minimal certificate has no removable item. A minimum-cost
    certificate is globally cheapest in the defined search space. Neither is
    necessarily cheapest to discover. Exact solver success establishes only
    the optimum and feasibility of its actual finite encoding. State timeout,
    candidate truncation and infeasibility honestly; absence of a certificate
    in a restricted representation does not imply real source insufficiency.

Recommended manuscript language

We formalize source-relative authorization of complete scoped answers from
positive support and exclusion of modeled incompatible interpretations. Our
finite solver is exact for supplied judgments and declared costs. Completeness
of the candidate set, soundness of source judgments, and source-to-world truth
are separate assumptions, not consequences of optimization. The resulting
framework specializes established certificate and active-discrimination ideas;
its empirical value depends on automatic witness discovery beyond matched
structural and generic sufficiency baselines.

Do not claim: first evidence certificate; first ambiguity-aware RAG; new
submodular objective for conjunctions; optimal end-to-end retrieval from an
offline evidence optimum; a calibrated risk guarantee from a finite beam; or
ACL-level theory novelty established solely by combining these ingredients.
