Source binding repair: design decision before natural predictions
Date: 2026-10-06
Status: source witness audit pending.
This document specifies a possible v26b repair.
It does not authorize natural model calls or claim methodological novelty.

Decision

Do not execute the current v26 query-frame comparison.
Its fields describe the requested subject, relation, scope, and temporal constraint.
Those fields often follow directly from the question.
Additional source evidence can change an answer without changing that query frame.
Consequently, stable frame annotations do not test source-fact revision.
They cannot establish a negative result for the canonical v25 hypothesis.
Preserve the protocol, configuration, references, packets, and tokenizer receipts as a pre-run design audit.
No natural model predictions supported this decision.
Do not retrospectively rename those frame annotations as source interpretations.

The intended object

The v25 hypothesis concerns interpretations of assertions inside retrieved source text.
A source assertion may bind a number or statement to an entity, population, or event time.
An extractor can bind that assertion incorrectly.
Additional source text may correct the binding.
The original assertion remains the object being interpreted.
A different answer, different assertion, or changed query frame is a different outcome.

Source binding requires entity, relation, value, unit, scope, and temporal assignment.
These describe the anchored assertion, rather than the property requested by the question.
Temporal assignment must distinguish event time, validity time, and publication time where relevant.
A publication date does not automatically establish a fact's validity interval.
Missing dates and provenance remain unknown.
Source binding alone does not establish that one fact supersedes another.
That claim additionally requires two identified assertions and a supported replacement relation.

Fixed material

Retain all 60 original questions and their original wording.
Retain the exact frozen C0 and C1 packets.
Retain all failures, exclusions, and uninteresting questions in the accounting.
The oversized v26-027 remains an execution failure under the frozen runtime budget.
Do not add passages, crop units, increase budgets, or replace questions to create witnesses.
The known-document pool remains a labelled diagnostic pool.
It does not establish corpus retrieval quality.
References may use only the delivered question and corresponding packet.
Released benchmark answers and omitted source passages cannot supply a missing premise.

Witness audit before implementation

Audit every frozen question for actual source assertions in C0.
Identify exact E## spans and their existing source offsets.
Record which entity, relation, value, unit, scope, or temporal binding the assertion requires.
Record any additional C1 unit that supplies a relevant binding premise.
Explain its contribution without inferring publication metadata from document order or identifiers.
Record whether C0 already determines the binding, leaves it unknown, or supports multiple readings.
Write the corresponding C1 judgment separately.
Unknown means unknown within the delivered packet.
It does not mean unknowable throughout the underlying source collection.

Every proposed witness requires an unchanged focal assertion across C0 and C1.
The added passage must clarify that assertion's binding or provide independent support for it.
A passage giving a separate answer does not satisfy this condition.
A date answering the question is not automatically a temporal binding correction.
A second claim with another value is not automatically a competing interpretation of the first claim.
Value extraction errors and entity, scope, or temporal misbindings must receive separate labels.

Do not require a wrong interpretation to remain defensible after reading C1.
The complete packet may clearly establish one binding.
Do not require C0 to mislead every competent reader.
Ordinary extraction errors remain possible even when C0 already contains sufficient evidence.
Such errors provide weaker evidence for an evidence-induced revision mechanism.
Their contribution must remain separate from corrections needing an added premise.

Anchor selection and disclosure

The cleanest inexpensive component test supplies a fixed source anchor to every arm.
The anchor contains an exact C0 quote and its E## location.
It supplies no interpreted entity, scope, date, answer, or replacement label.
All arms must interpret that same quoted assertion.
This is oracle-assisted anchor selection.
It isolates interpretation quality and does not establish automatic assertion proposal.

Source reviewers have already inspected both packets.
Do not claim their subsequent anchor selection was blind to C1.
Record the selection procedure and its access to both packets.
Keep the full 60-question denominator and all rejected anchor candidates.
Do not choose between multiple anchors using future model errors or improvements.
Freeze the anchor policy before any natural prediction.
Prefer one anchor per question for the minimal test.
If multiple anchors qualify, use one prespecified source-order rule.
Additional anchors remain descriptive and cannot inflate the main sample size.

An alternative asks the model to propose its own source assertion.
That alternative couples proposal, assertion identity, and interpretation quality.
It also complicates pre-prediction references and matched correction scoring.
Do not silently combine that harder task with the initial fixed-anchor diagnostic.

Minimal v26b output and reference contract

Use a new versioned schema and configuration if the audit justifies execution.
Do not reinterpret the current v26 scalar fields in place.
Each item needs a frozen anchor identifier, exact quote, source locator, and question identifier.
Each prediction needs status and at most two interpretations of that anchor.
Each interpretation records entity, relation, value, unit, scope, temporal assignment, and evidence identifiers.
Unknown and not-applicable remain distinct.
The temporal field must preserve source precision and identify its semantic role.
Require citations from Q and delivered E## labels only.
The source assertion must not change when a binding changes.

References independently describe the anchor under C0 and C1.
Freeze required fields, accepted values, and complete evidence sets before predictions.
All interpreted values remain hidden from model prompts.
The anchor quote already belongs to C0 and may be repeated for identification.
Count that repeated quote in every prompt's cost.
Freeze source-bounded resolved, ambiguous, insufficient, and ineligible status semantics.
Allow ambiguity only when reviewed source evidence supports incompatible bindings.
Model disagreement alone cannot establish source ambiguity.
Ledger quality remains a secondary mechanism diagnostic.

Matched arms

I0 interprets the fixed source anchor using the question and C0.
B1 performs fresh interpretation using C1 and the untrusted I0 draft.
R1 revises the same draft using the identical C1 evidence.
F1 interprets the same anchor using C1 without receiving I0.
O1 is the reviewed C1 reference, written before predictions.
O1 receives no evidence outside the corresponding packet.
All executable arms use the same model, decoding, output cap, and rendering.
B1 and R1 receive identical anchor information and previous-output bytes.
Charge I0 to both two-stage arms.
Report F1's cheaper one-call cost and the previously specified charged-I0 sensitivity.
Use native tokenizer checks for every new prompt.
Existing v26 token counts do not certify a changed v26b schema or prompt.

Correction and harm

An interpretation correction requires an asserted I0 binding that conflicts with the reviewed C1 binding.
The later prediction must correctly interpret the same anchored assertion.
Citation mismatch alone does not establish a semantic binding error.
Unknown-to-known transitions are evidence gain, not correction.
Extracting a newly supplied answer is answer gain, not source-binding correction.
Changing the focal assertion is target switching, not correction.
Unreviewed paraphrases require blinded adjudication before they can count as gains or harms.
Preserve strict scores and any later adjudicated scores separately.

Report entity, scope, and temporal misbinding corrections separately from value extraction corrections.
Report unsupported initial assertions separately from C0-supported wrong bindings.
Report whether the correction needs an added C1 premise.
Harm requires an initially correct, C0-supported binding that becomes wrong.
Unsupported resolution measures asserted certainty where C1 remains ambiguous or insufficient.
No correction count can hide a corresponding increase in harm.
Use question-level paired counts; multiple fields cannot become independent examples.

What would defeat this route

Full-context re-extraction is a strong explanation of any apparent improvement.
It has the same complete evidence and does not need to preserve initial errors.
The fixed anchor removes much of the proposal difficulty.
A sufficiently competent extractor may therefore solve this component without specialized revision.
If B1 matches R1, candidate-preserving prompting has no demonstrated benefit.
If F1 matches R1 more cheaply, revision has no practical advantage in this setting.
If F1 removes B1's errors, the residual concerns draft anchoring rather than necessary temporal revision.
If a stronger extractor removes the errors, small-reader weakness cannot support a specialized method claim.
If every improvement is unknown-to-known, the experiment measures evidence gain.
If anchors lack source-relative temporal meaning, this dataset does not instantiate the intended task.

A prompt improvement would remain a diagnostic observation.
It would not establish a novel learned algorithm.
Any later mechanism still needs comparison with ordinary structured prediction and existing revision methods.
The method must explain why maintaining candidate bindings improves correction without preserving false bindings.
The original harmful-supersession motivation requires downstream claim-pair and answer-level evidence.
A single-anchor success cannot supply that evidence.

Go and no-go decisions

First, finish the source witness audit before implementing v26b.
Report source-binding targets, added-premise witnesses, stable controls, and source-access failures across all 60 questions.
These are source judgments, not observed model errors.
They cannot satisfy the original error-count threshold.

Do not proceed if the available packets cannot support objective source-binding references.
Do not proceed if fields merely restate the question or copy the requested answer.
Do not proceed if qualifying anchors require generated questions, outside evidence, or post hoc packet edits.
Do not proceed if too few reviewed targets could possibly support the original 12-error gate.
Scarce added-premise witnesses should stop the evidence-induced revision claim before model spending.
Stable complete bindings may remain useful as controls, not as substitutes for that evidence.

If source witnesses justify execution, freeze v26b references and prompts before natural calls.
Then retain the original practical threshold: at least 12 source-resolvable initial interpretation errors.
Require at least six corrected oracle cases beyond ordinary B1 re-extraction.
Also require a meaningful residual against F1 before specialized mechanism development.
Unknown-to-known transitions cannot satisfy either error threshold.
These thresholds are feasibility rules, not statistical significance tests.
Do not lower them after observing results.

The outcome of the present v26 review is a corrected experimental target.
It is neither a demonstrated source-revision failure nor an empirical method win.
The project still lacks a verified novel algorithm and a held-out advantage.
