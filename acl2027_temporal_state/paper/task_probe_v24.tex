\section{A bounded source and retrieval diagnostic}
Before inspecting question-specific source outcomes, we fixed four natural-language
question templates across six exposed issuer histories (24 items, 12 filings).
These are authored development questions, not organically collected queries or
held-out data. Separate assistant roles authored and reviewed source references
blind to retrieval outcomes: nine questions had complete source support, ten
were partial, and five unresolved. Failure to locate a disclosure was not treated
as proof of global insufficiency.

Four conventional policies were evaluated at rendered-word caps of 1,024, 4,096,
and 16,384, with all headers and repeated text charged. Policies combined typed
cards, BM25, source-year routing, structural expansion and generic clause-query
fusion. No proposed certificate policy was evaluated. Source-year routing differed
between the lexical policy and the other policies; this is not a closure-only
ablation. Word limits are not model-token or total acquisition-cost limits.

At the primary cap, a separate assistant review inspected all 36 packs for the
nine source-supported complete questions. It accepted equivalent typed fields,
table fragments and alternative source passages. Policy identities were visible.
The following are evidence-sufficiency judgments, not reader predictions:

$^{*}$Typed cards expose no raw-text intervals; their exact-witness zero is an
interface property. Their semantic fields were reviewed separately.

The frozen reference conjunction and post-retrieval question-necessity
adjudication are both retained. In particular, extra royalty-history and
zero-outstanding-award details were not allowed to create failures for otherwise
answerable questions. An alternative cash-flow-statement witness also resolved
a preferred-anchor miss. All 24 source questions remain in the probe denominator;
the nine-item table is a descriptive, correlated, source-supported subset.

One segment question lacks evidence under all four implementations. Another
has an uncertain lexical outcome. Under a conservative interpretation of the
frozen two-history expansion rule, the latter does not count as a confirmed
residual, so the expansion gate is not passed. The observed misses do not show
failure of untested dense/hybrid retrieval or semantic query planning. Duration
facts omitted by the numeric-card profile are ordinary representation limitations.

The full 288-cell run and a separately frozen fixed-seed repeat selected identical
evidence in every cell; all 576 packs were hash- and budget-validated. An earlier
interrupted attempt and its subsequent blind software corrections are preserved.
Twenty focused authored software tests passed. No new model predictions, human
gold, automatic source-coverage guarantee, held-out effect or novel-method
advantage follows from this diagnostic. The natural reader gate and subsequent
proposer/verifier and confirmatory studies remain pending.
