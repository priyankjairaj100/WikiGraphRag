\section{Finite certificate optimization}
\label{sec:algorithm}

This section concerns a fixed, supplied witness map. Its exact optimum is a
packing diagnostic once judgments are available; it is not the acquisition
cost of discovering those judgments. The constructions are standard Boolean
optimization and cover arguments integrated with the grounding contract.
Their guarantees do not validate source semantics, candidate coverage, or
unobserved verifier outcomes.

\subsection{Actions, witnesses, and the optimization target}

Let $A$ be finitely many indivisible actions, $E_0$ the initially received
evidence, and $E(X)$ the union of anchored intervals received from $E_0$ and
actions $X\subseteq A$. Distinct immutable source versions never share an
interval. Each hypothesis $h\in H$ has a nonempty required-claim set $R_h$
and a complete joint answer action $a_q(h)$. Both versions of a requested
comparison belong to this same hypothesis. Answer classes here are equality
classes of normalized scoped actions; differing classes are incompatible.

A signed witness $w$ concludes $(h,c,+)$ or $(h,c,-)$ from a nonempty set
$P_w$ of anchored source premises. Write
\[
 z_w(X)=\mathbf 1\{P_w\text{ is covered by }E(X)\},\qquad
 p_{hc}(X)=\bigvee_{w:(h,c,+)}z_w(X),\quad
 n_{hc}(X)=\bigvee_{w:(h,c,-)}z_w(X).
\]
All premises must be received; names of selected actions have no evidential
meaning. Explicit unresolved judgments add neither sign. An active positive
and negative judgment on the same claim is a conflict, wherever it occurs.
For a chosen target $h$, the finite certificate constraints are
\begin{align}
 p_{hc}(X)&=1 &&(c\in R_h), \label{eq:alg-support}\\
 \sum_{c\in R_g}n_{gc}(X)&\geq1
       &&(g\in H:\ a_q(g)\neq a_q(h)), \label{eq:alg-rivals}\\
 p_{gc}(X)+n_{gc}(X)&\leq1 &&(g\in H,\ c\in R_g).
       \label{eq:alg-conflicts}
\end{align}
An independent cleared-coverage assessment is also necessary for
authorization. Refutation in \eqref{eq:alg-rivals} concerns \emph{every}
hypothesis in each rival class, not one class representative. Same-action
parses need not all have positive support. Zero-weight rivals are included.

For declared charged cost $C(X)$, minimize it over selections satisfying
\eqref{eq:alg-support}--\eqref{eq:alg-conflicts} for some $h$; the minimum is
$+\infty$ if none exists. Typed side constraints may separately limit
acquisition cost and actual rendered input. Unknown coverage permits a
\emph{conditional} packing optimum, with authorization false. It cannot be
cleared by optimization. A partial answer requires a separately declared
projected task, rather than deleting an inconvenient claim of the joint task.

\begin{proposition}[Equivalence to the finite answerability gate]
Under the answer-class contract, constraints
\eqref{eq:alg-support}--\eqref{eq:alg-conflicts}, cleared coverage, and the
budget constraints are equivalent to complete positive support for some
survivor, a nonempty survivor set, no incompatible surviving pair, and no
conflict or unresolved coverage blocker.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The target is supported and cannot be contradicted because of
\eqref{eq:alg-conflicts}; it therefore survives. Equation
\eqref{eq:alg-rivals} excludes every different-action hypothesis, leaving
compatible survivors. Conversely, choose a completely supported survivor
from an authorized selection. Every different-action hypothesis must have
been excluded by a negative required claim; the other constraints are the
remaining gates. These are finite algebraic statements, not proofs that the
supplied judgments or coverage assessment are correct.
\end{proof}

Pair loss may prioritize search but cannot replace these constraints: it can
vanish after eliminating everyone, before positive support, or in the
presence of zero-weight rivals.

\subsection{An exact Boolean formulation with shared cost}

Split each source at every action, initial-evidence, and premise endpoint,
obtaining nonempty elementary intervals $e\in V$. Let $A_e$ be actions
providing atom $e$, $b_e$ indicate initial coverage, and $V_w$ be the premise
atoms of witness $w$. Include unprovided gaps: an atom with $A_e=\varnothing$
cannot accidentally become received. This partition concerns coverage,
\emph{not} additive native token cost.

For binaries $x_a,u_e,z_w,p_{hc},n_{hc},t_h$, impose
\begin{align}
 u_e&=b_e\ \vee\!\bigvee_{a\in A_e}x_a,
 &z_w&=\bigwedge_{e\in V_w}u_e, \label{eq:alg-circuit}\\
 p_{hc}&=\bigvee_{w:(h,c,+)}z_w,
 &n_{hc}&=\bigvee_{w:(h,c,-)}z_w. \nonumber
\end{align}
An empty OR equals zero. Implement $y=\bigvee_{i\in I}v_i$ by
$y\geq v_i$ and $y\leq\sum_i v_i$; implement
$y=\bigwedge_{i\in I}v_i$ by $y\leq v_i$ and
$y\geq\sum_i v_i-|I|+1$. Then add
\begin{align}
 \sum_h t_h&=1,& p_{hc}&\geq t_h &&(h,c),\nonumber\\
 p_{hc}+n_{hc}&\leq1 &&&&(h,c),\label{eq:alg-milp}\\
 \sum_{c\in R_g}n_{gc}&\geq t_h
 &&&&(h,g:\ a_q(g)\neq a_q(h)).\nonumber
\end{align}
Both directions of each Boolean equivalence are essential: a solver must
not hide an adverse activated witness by choosing $z_w=0$.

For a nonnegative additive union-cost model, action $a$ uses charge resources
$Q_a$. Set $v_r=\bigvee_{a:r\in Q_a}x_a$, fixing already paid resources as
appropriate, and minimize
\begin{equation}
 C_0+\sum_r c_r v_r+\sum_a k_a x_a,
 \qquad c_r,k_a\geq0.\label{eq:alg-unioncost}
\end{equation}
A shared header resource is charged once; per-action cost $k_a$ is separately
charged. Resources and evidence atoms need not coincide. Different cost
units require separate constraints or an explicit scalar conversion.

\begin{proposition}[Exact finite formulation]
The binary program has exactly the evidence selections satisfying
\eqref{eq:alg-support}--\eqref{eq:alg-conflicts}. With the declared additive
resource contract, its objective is their exact charged cost.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For an action selection, the first equivalence forces the actual source
union; the ANDs force every witness's actual activation; the remaining ORs
force all positive and negative signs. Thus adverse judgments cannot be
suppressed. A binary target $t_h=1$ is possible precisely when all its claims
are supported, every different-action rival is contradicted, and all claims
are conflict-free. Conversely these conditions supply a feasible target and
all forced variables. Resource ORs charge precisely the resource union, while
the last term charges actions individually. The correspondence preserves
the objective. Coverage remains an external gate.
\end{proof}

If incompatibility is a general symmetric graph $I$, rather than the
complement of an equivalence relation, introduce survivor binaries
$s_h\leq1-n_{hc}$ for all $c$ and
$s_h\geq1-\sum_c n_{hc}$. Replace the rival constraints with
$s_h+s_g\leq1$ for $(h,g)\in I$ and $t_h\leq s_h$. Refuting only neighbors
of the chosen target could otherwise leave an incompatible pair among
\emph{other} survivors.

\subsection{Exact rendering and safe search}

The existing renderer merges adjacent intervals, potentially removing source
labels. Adding evidence can therefore reduce rendered cost; native
tokenization also need not be additive across fragments. Equation
\eqref{eq:alg-unioncost} represents actual prompt tokens only with a proved
renderer/tokenizer contract. Checking one incumbent does not establish that
contract globally.

A generic exact alternative uses the renderer and tokenizer as a deterministic
cost oracle. For each target, branch on action inclusion, maintaining selected
$S$, excluded actions, and undecided $U$. Prune a branch if $S$ already has a
conflict or contradicts the target, or if even $S\cup U$ lacks a required
target support or rival refutation. A conflict in $S\cup U$ alone is not a
reason to prune. A cost prune requires a valid lower bound on \emph{every}
completion. Under arbitrary rendering, $C(S)$ need not be such a bound;
zero is valid for a nonnegative oracle. Additive union costs permit $C(S)$
and linear-program relaxations as lower bounds. The same rule applies to
each hard side budget.

Whenever $S$ is a certificate, compare its exact cost with the incumbent.
With nonmonotone cost, continue exploring descendants: a larger certificate
may render more cheaply. Enumerate all unpruned leaves.

\begin{proposition}[Exact oracle search]
Complete search with these pruning rules returns a minimum finite
certificate for any deterministic nonnegative cost oracle.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every action subset is a leaf. Signed witness activation is monotone, so an
existing conflict persists. An existing target contradiction either persists
or becomes a prohibited conflict. If an obligation is absent from the union
of all allowed remaining actions, no completion satisfies it. These prunes
remove only infeasible completions. A valid cost lower bound removes only
completions unable to improve the incumbent. Every other feasible leaf is
compared with the exact objective; an optimum is retained or matched. The
argument does not assume monotone claim states in the presence of conflicts.
\end{proof}

Worst-case complexity is exponential. A time limit without an incumbent is
unknown feasibility, not infeasibility. With an incumbent, report its exact
cost and a valid global bound from unresolved branches or the exact MILP.
A surrogate-cost gap is not a native-token gap after post-hoc retokenization.
An arbitrary finite oracle can also be encoded by one variable per complete
action-set configuration and a precomputed cost table, but that exact MILP
has exponentially many configurations.

\section{Limits of myopic selection}
\label{sec:alg-limits}

\begin{proposition}[Classical set-cover embedding]
Minimum finite certificate cost is NP-hard even with singleton premises,
nonoverlapping additive action costs, no conflicts, and supplied cleared
coverage. The budget decision problem is NP-complete when costs are
polynomially evaluable.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Given weighted Set Cover with universe $U$, sets $S_i$, and costs $c_i$,
create target $h^*$ with one claim supported by anchored initial evidence,
and a rival $g_u$ for each $u\in U$. Rivals have different actions from
$h^*$ and no positive witnesses. Action $i$ costs $c_i$ and provides a unique
span whose singleton witnesses contradict exactly the rivals $g_u$ with
$u\in S_i$. There are no negative witnesses for $h^*$ or conflicts. Only
$h^*$ can be supported, so a selection is a certificate exactly when its
sets cover $U$, at identical incremental cost. A fixed initial charge can be
added to the decision budget. A proposed certificate is polynomially checked
by coverage, sign evaluation, and the stated cost algorithm, proving membership
in NP.
\end{proof}

This cost-preserving reduction inherits the classical Set Cover
approximation barrier \citep{dinur2014parallel}; it is not a new hardness
theorem. A fixed base charge can be made negligible by scaling action
costs. We do not identify the number of hypotheses, evidence atoms, and
total input size when importing a logarithmic hardness parameter.

\begin{proposition}[Conjunctive witnesses violate diminishing returns]
Refuted-rival count, obligation coverage, and fixed-weight pair-loss reduction
need not be submodular, even with two-premise witnesses and no conflicts.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $h^*$ be initially supported and $g$ an incompatible rival. Neither of
two actions $a,b$ produces a judgment alone, but together they activate a
negative witness for $g$. For the refutation indicator $f$,
\[
 f(\{b\})-f(\varnothing)=0
 \quad<\quad f(\{a,b\})-f(\{a\})=1.
\]
The same instance has one refutation obligation and one positive-weight
incompatible edge, giving the identical violation for the other objectives.
\end{proof}

The deterministic single-realization case already violates the required
marginal inequality, so adaptive submodularity is not automatic. EC2 analyzes
a specified Bayesian test-outcome model \citep{golovin2010ec2}; arbitrary
conjunctive source rules are not that model. Grouping complete premises into
tests changes the ground set and its cost. Moreover, corrected Theorem~13
and its historical note in \citet{golovin2011adaptive}, arXiv version~5,
replace a flawed earlier general expected-cover argument. We import no EC2
constant and do not claim that EC2 itself has been disproved.

Define a $k$-lookahead improvement policy to admit only moves adding at most
$k$ primitive actions that immediately reduce pair loss, increase supported
claims, or first enable answerability. Positive pair-loss density outranks
zero density. This includes the proposed improvement-only singleton and
two-bundle rules, but excludes deliberate zero-gain planning moves.

\begin{proposition}[No finite ratio for fixed improvement lookahead]
For every fixed $k\geq1$, this policy class has unbounded certificate-cost
ratio with premises of arity $k+1$, and can fail under a budget admitting a
certificate.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Initially $h^*$ is completely supported and one incompatible rival remains.
Actions $a_1,\ldots,a_{k+1}$ cost one each; a negative witness for the rival
requires all their spans. An alternative action $d$ costs $M>k+1$ and alone
refutes it. No additional positive-support progress is possible. Every move
of at most $k$ cheap actions has zero gain; every improving move contains
$d$ and costs at least $M$. A maximum-density policy chooses $d$ alone.
The optimal certificate costs $k+1$, so the ratio is at least $M/(k+1)$,
which diverges with $M$. A positive common initial charge preserves divergence.
With budget $B=k+1<M$, there is no admitted improving move although all cheap
actions form a feasible certificate. For two-bundle lookahead, three premises
suffice.
\end{proof}

This does not prohibit singleton actions in a globally planned algorithm.
Such a planner can intentionally buy a zero-gain premise. It also does not
deny that two-bundle lookahead repairs a particular two-part example.

\section{A restricted classical cover guarantee}
\label{sec:alg-cover}

Fix a complete target $h$. Obligations $O_h$ are its required positive claims
and one refutation for \emph{each} different-action hypothesis. Remove those
already satisfied by $E_0$ and let $m=|O_h|$. Assume the following explicitly
checkable finite-map conditions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)]
\item All eligible actions together activate no positive/negative conflict.
\item Each reachable useful witness has a unique inclusion-minimal activating
action set $D_w$, so $z_w(X)=1$ exactly when $D_w\subseteq X$ after conditioning
on $E_0$.
\item Incremental cost is the nonnegative union-resource cost
$c(X)=\sum_{r\in\cup_{a\in X}Q_a}c_r$, excluding previously paid resources.
\item The action pool and signed map are fixed and known. Their construction
and information-access costs are separately disclosed.
\end{enumerate}
Condition (A) is sufficient and deliberately strong. Under independently
selectable actions, (B) also has a direct polynomial check.

\begin{lemma}[Checking unique activation]
For a reachable witness, let $M_w$ be the set of actions that are sole
providers of some uncovered required atom. Its unique minimal activating
set exists exactly when $M_w$ covers all remaining uncovered required atoms;
then $D_w=M_w$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each sole provider belongs to every activating set. Conversely, if an action
is not a sole provider, selecting all other actions still covers every required
atom not already in $E_0$, so that action is not mandatory. If the finite family
of activating sets
has a unique inclusion-minimal member, every activating set contains it:
repeated deletion reaches a minimal member. That member therefore consists
exactly of the mandatory actions and must cover all remaining premises.
Conversely, if $M_w$ covers those premises, together with $E_0$ it activates
the witness and is contained in
every activating set, making it uniquely minimal.
\end{proof}

For every distinct nonempty useful $D_w$, create a macro $j$ with primitive
actions $D_w$, cost $c_j=c(D_w)$, and obligation coverage $Q_j$ obtained by
evaluating that union. Remove empty-coverage macros and duplicates. Let
$\rho$ be the maximum number of retained macros containing a positive-cost
resource; set $\rho=1$ if there are no positive-cost resources. These macros
are plans to purchase complete premises, not free summaries.

Apply weighted greedy set cover to the fixed $(Q_j,c_j)$: take useful
zero-cost macros first, otherwise minimize cost per newly covered obligation.
For this analysis use the \emph{full} macro cost, not an overlap-discounted
marginal cost. Finally purchase the union of the chosen primitive actions.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional cover bound]
Under (A)--(D), for a feasible target with $m\geq1$, greedy returns a
selection satisfying the support, refutation, and conflict constraints,
of incremental cost at most $\rho H_m\mathrm{OPT}_h$, where
$H_m=\sum_{i=1}^m1/i$. Returning the cheaper of this result and the union of
the cheapest macro for each obligation gives
\[
 c(X)\leq\min\{m,\rho H_m\}\,\mathrm{OPT}_h.
\]
For $m=0$, return the initial evidence without additional actions.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every obligation satisfied by an optimal primitive selection $X^*$ has an
active witness; (B) gives a macro $D_w\subseteq X^*$. These macros cover
$O_h$, and each resource of $X^*$ is charged in at most $\rho$ macros.
Thus an additive macro cover costs at most $\rho c(X^*)$.

For completeness, the standard weighted greedy argument
\citep{chvatal1979greedy} is as follows. With $r$ obligations uncovered, an
optimal additive cover of cost $T$ has total residual coverage at least $r$;
some set has cost per newly covered obligation at most $T/r$. Charge each
newly covered obligation the chosen ratio. The $j$th obligation covered
receives charge at most $T/(m-j+1)$, so total cost is at most $H_mT$.
The actual resource union costs no more than this sum of macro costs.

Alternatively, for each obligation its witness macro inside $X^*$ costs at
most $c(X^*)$; the cheapest eligible macro costs no more. Summing over $m$
obligations proves the $m$ bound. Either union covers every obligation and
is conflict-free by (A), hence satisfies the stated structural constraints.
\end{proof}

This is a classical cover corollary with explicit union-cost comparison,
not a new approximation theorem. Disjoint macro charge resources give
$\rho=1$. If (A)--(D) hold for every feasible target, choosing the cheapest
verified result gives the corresponding uniform bound against the global
optimum. Otherwise its comparator is restricted to eligible targets.

The coverage gate and actual budget must still pass. This approximation is
not budget-preserving: its output may exceed $B$ even when the optimum fits.
The guarantee is single-objective and has no extra hard side constraints.
For example, minimizing acquisition cost with a separate exact rendered-token
capacity is not covered: check that capacity and use the exact constrained
solver where needed. Failure of (A) can make a macro union conflict. Failure
of (B) requires enumerating all minimal premise-covering action sets, possibly
exponentially many; a shortlist supports only a shortlist-optimum guarantee.
Failure of (C) prevents calling the bound a native-token bound. Bounded
premise arity alone does not restore submodularity. Every bound remains
conditional on the supplied signed map and independent coverage assessment.
