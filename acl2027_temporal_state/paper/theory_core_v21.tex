\section{A finite certificate for complete scoped answers}
\label{sec:certificate-theory}

The following results specify a certificate contract. They assume source
semantics, query interpretation and coverage properties that the supplied-map
selector does not itself establish. In particular, exact checking of a finite
certificate is not a proof that a learned verifier is sound or that the
candidate set is complete.

\paragraph{Joint actions.}
Fix a question $q$, an immutable admitted corpus $D$, and a declared policy
$P$ for query readings, source authority, version identity, normalization and
permitted inference. Let $R_q$ be the finite ordered set of required output
slots. A result slot contains its value, concept, entity, period, unit/scale,
population, analysis definition and source-version identity. A comparison slot
also identifies its endpoint slots, relation type and material qualifiers.
Reporting two values, computing their difference, asserting a comparable
change and asserting an explicit correction are distinct actions. Comparison
claims require their own source premises or a registered checked derivation.

A hypothesis $h$ proposes a \emph{complete joint answer} for $R_q$, together
with a nonempty finite set of necessary source claims $C(h)$ and their binding
or derivation structure. Every material output claim and attachment must occur
in $C(h)$ or be derived explicitly from it. Let $K_q(h)$ state structural
completeness and faithful eligibility under the explicit question constraints.
The universe $\mathcal U$ contains eligible anchored joint interpretations;
the proposal set $H\subseteq\mathcal U$ is finite and nonempty. The universe
need not be finite or mechanically enumerable.

The canonical answer map $a_q(h)$ preserves every requested version and
comparison relation. Define
\begin{equation}
 h\sim_q g\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a_q(h)=a_q(g).
 \label{eq:answer-equivalence}
\end{equation}
This is an equivalence relation by equality of canonical records. Different
parse trees or supporting citations may be equivalent if they induce the same
scoped action. Equal numbers do not erase different entities, populations,
periods or source versions. For example, a question requesting original and
amended values has a two-version joint answer even when the values agree;
those versions are not rival hypotheses to be eliminated. A supplied symmetric
incompatibility graph is insufficient unless it faithfully separates distinct
actions. In particular, a nontransitive ``compatible enough'' relation does
not define the quotient in~\eqref{eq:answer-equivalence}.

\paragraph{Source consistency and warrant.}
Let $\mathcal M(D)$ be a nonempty class of models satisfying the admitted source
assertions under $P$. Provenance-scoped assertions such as ``version one reports
12'' and ``version two reports 15'' can both hold. Genuine source conflict must
be represented explicitly or excluded from this coherent-model theorem;
classical inconsistency cannot be used to entail arbitrary answers. For
$\phi_h=\bigwedge_{c\in C(h)}c$, define
\begin{align}
 T&=\{h\in\mathcal U:\exists m\in\mathcal M(D),\ m\models\phi_h\},
 \label{eq:consistent-hypotheses}\\
 W&=\{h\in\mathcal U:D\models\phi_h\}.
 \label{eq:warranted-hypotheses}
\end{align}
We consider resolution tasks with $T\ne\varnothing$. Since
$\mathcal M(D)\ne\varnothing$, $W\subseteq T$. Thus $T$ contains eligible
source-consistent joint interpretations, while $W$ contains proposals whose
required claims are all positively warranted. There may be multiple admissible
actions in $T$, including different readings of an underspecified question.

Source warrant and unique query resolution are deliberately separate. If
``positive support'' were defined to mean that an output is already the
uniquely correct full answer under every admissible reading, support alone
would establish correctness and the coverage theorem would be redundant. Our
support relation warrants the claims of a complete eligible proposal; the
certificate additionally establishes uniqueness over $T$. Assurance is
relative to $D,q,P$, not to undeclared sources, source authenticity, real-world
truth, or first-public availability.

\subsection{Conjunctive witness semantics}

A source span identifies an immutable source, exact offsets or coordinates,
and text identity. A witness rule $w$ names a candidate $h$, a necessary claim
$c\in C(h)$, a sign $\sigma\in\{+,-\}$, a nonempty finite dependency set
$D_w$ of source spans, and a derivation/provenance record. If $\operatorname{cov}(E)$
is the union of actually delivered spans, activation requires
\begin{equation}
 w\text{ active in }E
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{every span in }D_w\text{ is covered by }\operatorname{cov}(E).
 \label{eq:conjunctive-activation}
\end{equation}
All leaf dependencies of a multi-step derivation must be included. Bundle names,
nearby tokens, and generated explanations do not replace missing premises.

A rule is semantically sound when its anchored assertions jointly entail $c$
for sign $+$, or $\neg c$ for sign $-$, under $P$. Faithful extraction then gives
$D\models c$ or $D\models\neg c$, respectively. Presence of valid offsets and
hashes checks source identity and coverage, not that entailment.

Write $P_E(h,c)$ and $N_E(h,c)$ for the existence of an active positive and
negative rule. The claim is supported when $P_E\land\neg N_E$, contradicted
when $N_E\land\neg P_E$, and unresolved otherwise. Record a separate conflict
flag for $P_E\land N_E$. Missing evidence, retrieval failure and lack of proof
remain unresolved; they never imply contradiction. Define
\begin{align}
 \operatorname{Pos}_E(h)
 &\Longleftrightarrow \text{all }c\in C(h)\text{ are supported},\\
 \operatorname{Ref}_E(h)
 &\Longleftrightarrow \text{some }c\in C(h)\text{ is contradicted},\\
 V_E&=\{h\in H:\neg\operatorname{Ref}_E(h)\}.
\end{align}
The conservative certificate rejects any active claim conflict, including one
on a rival candidate. Harmless same-action survivors need not all be supported.

\begin{lemma}[Local semantic soundness]
\label{lem:local-soundness}
If all active rules are sound and $\mathcal M(D)$ is nonempty, no active claim
has a positive/negative conflict. Further,
$\operatorname{Pos}_E(h)$ implies $h\in W$, and
$\operatorname{Ref}_E(h)$ implies $h\notin T$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A conflict would entail both $c$ and $\neg c$ in every member of a nonempty
$\mathcal M(D)$, which is impossible. If each necessary claim is supported,
every model satisfies every conjunct of $\phi_h$, so $D\models\phi_h$. If one
necessary claim $c$ is contradicted, every model satisfies $\neg c$ and none
can satisfy $\phi_h$, which contains $c$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Coverage and certificate soundness}

\begin{definition}[Valid answer-class coverage]
\label{def:coverage}
The candidate set has coverage, denoted $\operatorname{COV}(H;D,q)$, when
\begin{equation}
 \forall t\in T\ \exists h\in H\cap T:
 a_q(h)=a_q(t).
 \label{eq:coverage}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}
This requires a \emph{source-consistent} representative for every admissible
answer class. Merely placing every action label in $a_q(H)$ is insufficient:
an invalid representative can be soundly refuted while a valid omitted
interpretation with the same action remains. Coverage of one reference
interpretation is also weaker than~\eqref{eq:coverage} for an ambiguous task.

An explicit unknown sentinel begins \textsc{open}. An operational decision
$\widehat{\operatorname{COV}}$ sets it to \textsc{covered} only through a declared
coverage assessment. Soundness of this assessment means
$\widehat{\operatorname{COV}}\Rightarrow\operatorname{COV}$. Beam exhaustion,
low pair loss, confidence, or removal of all proposed rivals is not a coverage
proof. A finite formal grammar may permit exhaustive domain-relative
enumeration; a natural-language audit or estimator remains fallible and must
be evaluated. The unknown sentinel is not an ordinary candidate that a finite
negative witness can eliminate. Its justification is logically separate, not
assumed statistically independent of other components.

\begin{definition}[Supported unique-answer certificate]
\label{def:certificate}
For exact declared evidence cost $\operatorname{cost}(E)$ and budget $B$, a
certificate chooses $E$ and a winner $h^*\in H$ satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item $\operatorname{cost}(E)\le B$;
 \item there is no active positive/negative claim conflict;
 \item $\operatorname{Pos}_E(h^*)$;
 \item every $g\in H$ with $a_q(g)\ne a_q(h^*)$ satisfies
       $\operatorname{Ref}_E(g)$;
 \item $\widehat{\operatorname{COV}}$ holds.
\end{enumerate}
The certificate records one complete support proof for $h^*$, an explicit
refutation for every rival candidate, and coverage-assessment provenance.
\end{definition}
Every member of each rival class must be refuted, unless a sound universal
class-level argument covers them all. Refuting one representative does not
refute a class. Positive support implies a nonempty survivor set; the gate is
equivalently a fully supported survivor plus agreement of all survivors, with
conflict, coverage and cost conditions retained. A zero weighted objective
does not replace this logical condition, particularly with zero-weight rivals
or an empty survivor set.

\begin{theorem}[Source-relative certificate soundness]
\label{thm:soundness}
Assume faithful query/schema and answer mapping, faithful source extraction
and output rendering, a nonempty coherent $\mathcal M(D)$, sound active witness
rules, and a sound coverage sentinel. A certificate as in
Definition~\ref{def:certificate} establishes $h^*\in W$ and
\begin{equation}
 \{a_q(t):t\in T\}=\{a_q(h^*)\}.
 \label{eq:unique-action}
\end{equation}
It therefore authorizes a complete positively warranted joint answer with a
unique action in the admitted source/query domain.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Positive support and Lemma~\ref{lem:local-soundness} imply $h^*\in W\subseteq T$;
the left side of~\eqref{eq:unique-action} thus contains the winning action.
For any $t\in T$, sound coverage supplies $h\in H\cap T$ with
$a_q(h)=a_q(t)$. If this differed from $a_q(h^*)$, the rival-refutation gate
would imply $\operatorname{Ref}_E(h)$; Lemma~\ref{lem:local-soundness} would then
give $h\notin T$, a contradiction. Every admissible interpretation therefore
has the winning action. Its complete support derivation warrants each required
claim, faithful schema checking includes every requested slot, and faithful
rendering adds no assertion. The budget condition establishes only the
declared evidence-cost bound and plays no role in semantic entailment.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Reference-target preservation]
\label{cor:target-preservation}
Fix $t^*\in T$. Replace full coverage in Theorem~\ref{thm:soundness} by the
existence of $h\in H\cap T$ with $a_q(h)=a_q(t^*)$. An accepted output remains
positively warranted and equals $a_q(t^*)$, but the premise does not establish
uniqueness over all of $T$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
If the output differed from the valid representative of $t^*$, that candidate
would be refuted, contradicting local soundness. Positive warrant follows as
before. No premise supplies a representative for any other admissible class,
so no conclusion about those classes follows.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite checking and conditional completeness}

Let $\mathcal A$ be a finite set of admissible source-span bundles and let the
rule list be finite. A selection $E\subseteq\mathcal A$ delivers the union of
its spans, which can activate a rule across multiple bundles. Let
$\mathcal F_B$ be the selections satisfying Definition~\ref{def:certificate}
for some winner, with all supplied objects and the cost renderer fixed.

\begin{proposition}[Exact finite-map checking]
\label{prop:finite-checking}
Exhaustive evaluation of all $E\subseteq\mathcal A$ returns a certificate if
and only if $\mathcal F_B\ne\varnothing$. If
\begin{equation}
 B^*=\min\{\operatorname{cost}(E):E\text{ satisfies gates 2--5}\},
\end{equation}
with $B^*=\infty$ for an empty set, a finite-budget certificate exists exactly
when $B\ge B^*$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The finite power-set enumeration includes every permitted selection. Each
selection is checked against the defining predicates of $\mathcal F_B$, so a
returned member is feasible and every feasible member is eventually checked.
When the set defining $B^*$ is nonempty, finiteness ensures the minimum is
attained. The budget equivalence follows from this definition, without any
assumption that cost is monotone under adding bundles.
\end{proof}
This establishes exactness on supplied maps, not semantic truth. A heuristic
failure does not prove infeasibility. Minimizing pair loss while allowing
abstention does not solve the certificate-existence problem.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional semantic completeness]
\label{thm:complete}
Assume the soundness premises of Theorem~\ref{thm:soundness}, and suppose
$T/{\sim_q}$ has one class with action $a^*$. Further suppose:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item the justified coverage sentinel is \textnormal{\textsc{covered}};
 \item some $h^*\in H$ with action $a^*$ has an expressible sound positive
       witness for every claim in $C(h^*)$;
 \item every $g\in H$ with action different from $a^*$ has an expressible
       sound negative witness for a necessary claim;
 \item a single admitted selection $E_0$ includes all these witness
       dependencies and satisfies $\operatorname{cost}(E_0)\le B$;
 \item search is complete over the admitted finite selections.
\end{enumerate}
Then the procedure returns a certificate with action $a^*$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The joint dependency condition activates the selected positive and negative
witnesses. Coherent global rule soundness excludes opposing active judgments
by Lemma~\ref{lem:local-soundness}. Thus $E_0$ supports all claims of $h^*$,
refutes every different-action candidate, is conflict-free and fits the
budget. The covered sentinel supplies the remaining gate. Hence
$\mathcal F_B\ne\varnothing$; complete search and
Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-checking} return a certificate.
Theorem~\ref{thm:soundness} gives its unique action $a^*$.
\end{proof}

Semantic uniqueness alone implies none of the witness-expressibility,
retrieval, verifier-recognition, joint-budget, coverage-assessment or
search-completeness premises. Individually affordable witnesses need not fit
together. This theorem therefore provides no completeness guarantee for an
unrestricted language model or the greedy supplied-map selector.

A predeclared projection $\pi$ onto a subset of requested slots gives an
analogous theorem with answer map $\pi\circ a_q$ and complete support for the
emitted projected claims. Alternatives differing only on withheld slots need
not be separated. The resulting certificate is a partial answer and does not
establish the omitted version or comparison relation.

\subsection{Failure localization and selective risk}

Consider a distribution over tasks and full adaptive runs. Let $A$ denote
emission of a complete answer and let $\mathrm{Bad}$ denote failure of the
grounded unique-resolution conclusion. Retain the nonempty coherent source-model
and eligible-domain setup. Initially assume exact query/action representation,
source extraction, schema and rendering. Require every emitted run to pass the
finite mechanical gate of Definition~\ref{def:certificate} and retain the
selected support and refutation proofs. This protocol premise does not assume
that the supplied semantic judgments or coverage assertion are true; their
failures are the error events below. On emitted runs,
let $E_C$ be a cleared but false coverage assertion, $E_+$ a false positive
judgment in the selected complete support proof, and $E_-$ a false negative
judgment used to eliminate a source-consistent rival. Here ``false negative''
means erroneous contradiction, not failure to recognize support; reporting
should use the clearer terms \emph{false support} and \emph{false elimination}.

\begin{theorem}[Error localization and risk bound]
\label{thm:risk}
Under those exact-component and mechanically gated emission assumptions,
without assuming correctness of the supplied semantic judgments or coverage
assertion,
\begin{equation}
 A\cap\mathrm{Bad}\subseteq E_C\cup E_+\cup E_-.
 \label{eq:failure-localization}
\end{equation}
With $E_V=E_+\cup E_-$,
\begin{align}
 \Pr(A\cap\mathrm{Bad})
 &\le \Pr(A\cap E_C)
     +\Pr(A\cap E_C^c\cap E_V)\nonumber\\
 &\le \Pr(E_C)+\Pr(E_+)+\Pr(E_-).
 \label{eq:marginal-risk}
\end{align}
If $p=\Pr(A)>0$, selective risk satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \Pr(\mathrm{Bad}\mid A)
 \le \min\left\{1,
 \frac{\Pr(E_C)+\Pr(E_+)+\Pr(E_-)}{p}\right\}.
 \label{eq:selective-risk}
\end{equation}
No independence assumptions are required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take an emitted run outside $E_C\cup E_+\cup E_-$. Its selected complete
positive proof is sound, so the winner is warranted. Correct coverage
represents every source-consistent action by a source-consistent candidate.
If any such action differed from the winner, the gate would have eliminated
its valid representative, requiring a false elimination and contradicting
the complement of $E_-$. Thus the answer is grounded and uniquely resolved,
proving~\eqref{eq:failure-localization}. Partition $A\cap\mathrm{Bad}$ into
the disjoint part in $E_C$ and the part in $E_C^c$. By localization the latter
lies in $E_V$. Taking probabilities and applying the union bound yields
\eqref{eq:marginal-risk}. Dividing the joint error by $\Pr(A)=p$ yields
\eqref{eq:selective-risk}.
\end{proof}

When $p=0$, selective risk is undefined; zero joint error from always
abstaining does not establish answer quality. Marginal omission/verifier
rates cannot be reported as answered-risk bounds without the denominator or
conditional calibration: answering on one percent of tasks and being wrong
every time gives joint error $0.01$ and selective risk $1$.

If directly justified conditional quantities are available, put
$\delta_C=\Pr(E_C\mid A)$ and
$\delta_V=\Pr(E_V\mid A\cap E_C^c)$. A partition gives
\begin{equation}
 \Pr(\mathrm{Bad}\mid A)
 \le\delta_C+(1-\delta_C)\delta_V.
\end{equation}
When the second conditioning event has zero probability its contribution is
zero and no conditional value is needed. The theorem estimates none of these
quantities; sampling, confidence and transfer assumptions remain empirical.

If query parsing, necessary-claim completeness, answer equivalence, extraction,
concept normalization or final rendering can fail, introduce $E_R$ for these
errors. Localization becomes
\begin{equation}
 A\cap\mathrm{Bad}\subseteq E_R\cup E_C\cup E_+\cup E_-.
\end{equation}
The risk bound must gain the corresponding term. A two-term candidate/verifier
bound is legitimate only after stating which other components are exact or
included in the verifier event. For a fixed reference endpoint,
Corollary~\ref{cor:target-preservation} permits target-class coverage in place
of full coverage, but does not certify absence of other admissible actions.

\paragraph{Adaptive calls.}
If at most $K$ judgments are issued and, for every reachable prior history,
the conditional probability that the next issued judgment is semantically
wrong is at most $\eta$, then the probability of any erroneous call is at most
$\min(1,K\eta)$. To see this, condition each issued-error indicator on its
prior history, bound its expectation by $\eta$, and apply the union bound.
With a bounded predictable stopping rule the same argument gives
$\eta\,\mathbb E[N]$ for the number $N$ of issued calls. Static average
per-call accuracy does not imply the required uniform conditional bound;
adaptive acquisition can concentrate on high-error cases. Counting all issued
calls is conservative and avoids conditioning only on favorable selected
judgments.

\subsection{Counterexamples and limits}

\begin{proposition}[Coverage cannot be inferred from a finite survivor set]
\label{prop:coverage-impossibility}
Without a sound coverage or equivalent uniqueness premise, a procedure
restricted to a finite candidate/judgment transcript cannot guarantee unique
resolution over all source-consistent completions of that transcript.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take the transcript $H=\{h_a\}$ with a fully supported $h_a$ and no modeled
rival. In one admissible completion, $T=\{h_a\}$. In a second,
$T=\{h_a,h_b\}$ with $a_q(h_b)\ne a_q(h_a)$; all supporting claims of $h_a$
remain true. The unrepresented possibility was not examined, so the visible
transcripts coincide. A deterministic procedure emits the same unique action
in both, making a false uniqueness claim in the second. Condition a randomized
procedure on its randomness and the same argument applies. If it emits with
probability $r$ on this transcript, its uniqueness claim fails with probability
$r$ in the second completion. Universal soundness requires abstention here or
an additional premise that distinguishes the completions.
\end{proof}
This does not make the supported facts of $h_a$ false. A perfect oracle for
full task correctness already solves factual correctness; the impossibility
concerns additional uniqueness with source-claim support. If support omits a
necessary binding, a missing correct candidate can produce a wrong answer,
but that construction also violates the complete-support contract.

\paragraph{Semantic judgments are essential.}
Let the source report 7 and let $H$ contain full candidates for 7 and 9.
Coverage is complete. False signed rules supporting 9 and refuting 7 can carry
valid offsets and hashes, activate under every syntactic requirement and make
the gate accept 9. Thus source identity cannot replace semantic soundness.
Alternatively, a false elimination can remove one genuinely grounded query
reading and produce false uniqueness while the selected winner's source
facts remain correct. Evaluating positive support alone cannot validate
refutation behavior.

\paragraph{Why the remaining gates are required.}
A singleton survivor without evidence for its unit or population has no
complete grounded support. Refuting every candidate also yields zero pair
loss but no answer. A refuted member of a rival class leaves an unrefuted
same-action member alive. A continuation-plus-header rule activated from the
header alone has lost a necessary dependency. Two separately requested source
versions cannot be collapsed into one action because their values agree.
Each construction violates a different explicit gate or representation
condition; none is repaired by increasing an objective weight.

\paragraph{Conflicts and monotonicity.}
With fallible supplied rules, adding evidence may activate positive support on
a previously contradicted claim. Its state becomes unresolved with a conflict,
the candidate reappears and authorization is blocked. Pair loss can increase;
answerability is not unconditionally monotone. Under globally sound rules and
coherent semantics, Lemma~\ref{lem:local-soundness} excludes conflicts and
negative conclusions persist as evidence is added, although budget feasibility
need not persist.

The unproved components remain automatic query-faithful candidate generation,
semantic source verification, natural-language coverage assessment, practical
search, calibrated selective error and utility beyond strong structural and
sufficiency controls at matched total costs. The certificate theory does not
establish any of these empirical premises.
